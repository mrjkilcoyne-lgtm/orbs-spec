# Physics Fundamentals

## Scope
Mechanics, thermodynamics, and electromagnetism: conservation laws, force, energy, momentum, and dimensional analysis.

## Core principles
- Force causes acceleration (F=ma); energy is conserved (kinetic, potential, heat); momentum is conserved in closed systems. These three principles solve most classical mechanics.
- Thermodynamics: heat flows from hot to cold (second law—entropy increases), energy is conserved (first law), and absolute zero is unattainable (third law). Entropy is a upper bound on information.
- Work is force × distance; power is work per time; efficiency is output/input. These apply to mechanical systems, fluids, and electrical systems interchangeably.
- Dimensional analysis: every equation must balance in units; checking dimensions catches algebra errors and guides approximation.
- Fields (electric, magnetic, gravitational) encode how one object influences others at a distance; Maxwell's equations unify electricity and magnetism.

## Apex practices
- Use energy methods (conservation) before forces; identify initial and final states, calculate potential/kinetic energy change, and solve for unknowns.
- Distinguish reversible (ideal, maximum work) from irreversible (real, wasted energy as heat) processes; efficiency gaps reveal where energy is lost.
- Leverage dimensional analysis to constrain functional forms: if output depends on inputs with known dimensions, the functional form is largely determined.
- Approximate: ignore friction, air resistance, or relativistic effects if they're small; dimensional arguments often show when approximations fail.

## Pitfalls
- Forgetting that energy is a state function (path-independent) while work is path-dependent; a process depends on how it's done, not just start/end.
- Misapplying momentum conservation when external forces act (true for closed systems only); verify the system boundary.
- Ignoring that thermodynamic efficiency has strict limits (Carnot efficiency); perpetual machines and magical efficiency breaks do not exist.

## Tools & references
Feynman Lectures on Physics (especially Volumes 1-2), Goldstein's "Classical Mechanics," Landau-Lifshitz series.
