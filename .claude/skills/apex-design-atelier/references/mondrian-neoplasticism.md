# Mondrian & Neoplasticism

## Scope
De Stijl's reduction to horizontal/vertical, primaries, and non-color — the grid as belief system, and its direct line into modern layout.

## Core principles
- Mondrian's system is a small closed grammar: horizontal + vertical lines, red/yellow/blue, black/white/grey — infinite compositions from five constraints. The lesson: expressive power comes from the severity of the ruleset, not its size.
- Asymmetric equilibrium: balance without symmetry — a large quiet field offsets a small intense one. This is the actual skill in every layout: weights balancing across an off-center fulcrum.
- The grid is not neutral: Mondrian adjusted line weights and intervals optically, by eye, for years per canvas. Systems set the possibility space; judgment places the elements.
- Relationships are the content: no shape matters alone; the intervals between lines are the composition. In UI terms — whitespace is not absence, it's the medium.
- Dynamic tension over static rest ("Broadway Boogie Woogie"): even a right-angle grammar can dance — rhythm comes from varied interval, not varied vocabulary.

## Apex practices
- Adopt the closed-grammar exercise: design a real screen with one typeface, two weights, three sizes, primaries only — severity exposes whether your composition skill or your palette was carrying you.
- Balance by value-mass: squint until elements are weight-blobs; move blobs until the off-axis balance holds, then restore detail.
- Tune the grid optically after setting it mathematically — Mondrian's hand-adjusted lines are the license to nudge the 8px system when the eye demands it.
- Let one interval be irregular on purpose: uniform spacing is wallpaper; a single syncopation makes the system felt.

## Pitfalls
- Mondrian-as-colorway: red/yellow/blue rectangles slapped on (the dress, the mug) while ignoring the equilibrium discipline that was the entire point.
- Perfect symmetry mistaken for balance — it's balance's dead relative.
- Grids obeyed into monotony: the system was made to be composed within, not to compose for you.

## Tools & references
