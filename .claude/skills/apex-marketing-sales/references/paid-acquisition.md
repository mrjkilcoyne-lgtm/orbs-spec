# Paid Acquisition

## Scope
Paid advertising to drive customer acquisition: channel selection, creative testing, bid management, and CAC optimization.

## Core principles
- Ad unit economics (CAC vs. LTV) determine viability; if LTV is $5,000 and CAC is $800, a 6x payback is achievable. If LTV is $5,000 and CAC is $4,000, payback is marginal and scaling is impossible.
- Channel-market fit matters; B2C social (Facebook, TikTok) works for impulse purchases (viral), while B2B technical (LinkedIn, Google search) works for high-consideration purchases.
- Spend cap per user (avoid overspending on late-stage audiences) prevents CAC deterioration; many companies burn margins by repeatedly advertising to the same person late in funnel.
- Creative decay (the same ad becomes invisible after 50k impressions) means continuous testing; the winning creative becomes a baseline, not the final answer.
- Attribution (which touchpoint drove the purchase?) is the hardest problem in paid; multi-touch attribution models claim to solve it but have limitations. Last-click attribution (give all credit to the last touchpoint) is simple but biased.

## Apex practices
- Start with one channel (Google Search for high intent, Facebook for brand awareness) and nail unit economics before scaling to others.
- Test incrementally: test one variable per test (audience, creative, offer, landing page) so you know what moved the needle.
- Build a lookalike audience (users similar to your best customers) and exclude converters (don't re-advertise to customers) to reduce wasted spend.
- Run continuous creative testing; allocate 10-20% of spend to testing new creatives, then refresh winners before saturation.

## Pitfalls
- Optimizing for clicks or impressions instead of conversions; cheap clicks that don't convert waste money.
- Scaling too fast; a channel that works at $1k/day might collapse at $10k/day because audience saturation drives up CAC.
- Ignoring holdout groups (A/B testing always on vs. off); you risk attributing business that would have happened anyway to the campaign.

## Tools & references
