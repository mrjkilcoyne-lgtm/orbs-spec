# Game Design

## Scope
Defining rules, progression, feedback systems, and emotional arc: designing for fun, engagement, and player agency across genres and platforms.

## Core principles
- A game is a series of meaningful choices with immediate, clear feedback — if a player action doesn't matter (choice doesn't change outcome) or feedback is unclear (player can't tell if they succeeded), it's not a game, it's an animation.
- Fun emerges from the gap between player skill and challenge (flow theory): too easy is boring, too hard is frustrating; dynamic difficulty or level design that scales challenge is the answer.
- Feedback loops (positive: earning rewards accelerates progress; negative: resource scarcity forces tradeoffs) drive moment-to-moment engagement and long-term pacing.
- Player agency (control, ownership, meaningful failure) is essential; a linear cutscene feels like you're watching someone else play.
- Core loop (the repeated action that defines the game: jump-attack-collect in platformers, explore-fight-loot in roguelikes) must be satisfying in isolation; everything else is dressing.

## Apex practices
- Playtest frequently, watch people (not yourself) play, and listen for the phrase "I didn't understand..." — that's design failure, not player failure.
- Design on paper first (game mechanics, win conditions, resource flows) before programming; many ideas fail at the whiteboard, saving months of dev time.
- Prototype the core loop in one day (bare-bones version) and iterate on it 10 times; shipped games are iterated core loops, not first ideas.
- Balance asymmetry: not every option should be equally viable, but every option should be viable for someone; dominant strategies are boring.

## Pitfalls
- Overloading complexity early; the first hour should teach and be fun, not bury players in systems.
- Design-by-committee or adding every idea a playteser suggests; prioritize player confusion (design issue) over nice-to-have features (scope creep).
- Ignoring pacing; three hours of the same loop is tedious, even if the loop is fun.

## Tools & references
Crawford's "The Art of Interactive Design," Swink's "Game Feel," Pressman's "The Art of Game Design," Fullerton's "Game Design Workshop," MDA framework (Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics).
