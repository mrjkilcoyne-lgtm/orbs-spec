# Product-Led Growth

## Scope
Using the product as the primary acquisition and activation tool: freemium models, self-service onboarding, viral mechanics, and land-and-expand.

## Core principles
- Product-led growth (PLG) inverts the sales motion: the product is the sales team. Instead of talking someone into buying, give them the product free or cheap, and let them upgrade when they see value.
- PLG works best for self-serve products (pricing tools, design apps, pairing software) where the buyer can get value in minutes without sales involvement.
- Freemium (free tier, paid tiers with more features/usage) is not PLG; it's a pricing model. PLG is using the free/trial product to acquire and convert customers.
- Viral loops (network effects, referral incentives, word-of-mouth) amplify PLG; Slack's growth was 90% word-of-mouth inside companies (viral), not top-down sales.
- PLG has a "aha moment" (the moment the user sees value and commits to the product); onboarding must lead users to the aha moment in minutes, not weeks.

## Apex practices
- Map the user journey from signup to paid to expansion and identify drop-off points; optimize the highest-leverage conversions (signup → aha moment → paid).
- Measure viral coefficient (the number of new users acquired per existing user) and retention curves (what % of free users are active 7 days, 30 days, 90 days after signup).
- Build referral incentives (give a month free for referring a paid user) and make sharing frictionless (copy a link, email an invite, add to Slack).
- Segment free users by "health score" (engagement, feature usage, company profile) and use it to prioritize upsell conversations (high-health users are 5x more likely to convert).

## Pitfalls
- Assuming PLG is "free is better"; many PLG companies have actually expensive free tiers and still find distribution. Free is not the strategy, user value is.
- Neglecting the bottom funnel; great product acquisition means nothing if you have no motion to convert free to paid (which takes sales or clear monetization).
- Letting product teams own growth without commercial discipline; product teams optimize for engagement, not revenue.
