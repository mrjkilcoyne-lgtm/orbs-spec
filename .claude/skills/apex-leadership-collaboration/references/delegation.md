# Delegation

## Scope
Transferring ownership of work and decisions to others in a way that grows people and scales the leader.

## Core principles
- Delegate outcomes, not tasks: "own our staging reliability" grows an engineer; "run these three scripts weekly" creates a human cron job.
- The delegation dial has levels (do exactly this → recommend, I decide → decide and inform me → decide, don't inform); most delegation failures are two people assuming different levels, so name the level out loud.
- Monkey management (Oncken & Wass, HBR "Who's Got the Monkey?"): every "quick question, what should I do?" is an attempt to transfer the problem back to you — coach the decision, never adopt the monkey.
- Delegate before you're comfortable: if you only hand off what someone is already proven at, nobody ever grows; expect the first iteration to be 70% of your quality and treat the gap as tuition.
- Accountability is not delegable — you can delegate authority and ownership, but when it fails publicly, "I delegated that" is never an acceptable sentence from the leader.

## Apex practices
- Use a written handoff for anything significant: outcome, constraints, decision authority, check-in cadence, and what "call for help" looks like — ambiguity here is where delegation dies.
- Match stretch to safety: delegate high-stakes-for-you, moderate-stakes-for-the-org work first (an internal tool launch, not the board demo).
- When reviewing delegated work, critique against the agreed outcome, not against "how I would have done it" — style corrections teach people to guess your taste instead of exercising judgment.
- Keep a "things only I can do" list and audit it quarterly; each item is either genuinely non-delegable (rare) or a training plan waiting to be written.

## Pitfalls
- Delegating the task but keeping every decision — the person becomes your typist and you remain the bottleneck with extra steps.
- Snatching work back at the first wobble ("faster to do it myself"), which is true this week and false every week after.
- Delegating only grunt work while keeping all visible, career-making work for yourself; people notice within one cycle.

## Tools & references
Oncken & Wass "Management Time: Who's Got the Monkey?" (HBR), the 7 Levels of Delegation (Management 3.0 delegation poker), "Turn the Ship Around!" (Marquet) for intent-based leadership.
