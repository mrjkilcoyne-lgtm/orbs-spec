# Engineering Management

## Scope
The transition from IC to manager and the core operating system of managing engineers: output, leverage, and organizational health.

## Core principles
- A manager's output = the output of their team plus the output of teams they influence (Grove, "High Output Management"); your own coding is rarely the highest-leverage hour.
- Management is a career change, not a promotion — the skills that made you a strong IC (depth, personal throughput) actively mislead you when the job becomes leverage through others.
- The manager's schedule vs. maker's schedule (Graham): every meeting you place fragments an engineer's half-day; batch interruptions and defend contiguous focus blocks.
- Span of control has real limits — beyond ~7 direct reports, one-on-ones, feedback quality, and career development all degrade measurably; hire or promote leads before you hit it.
- Task-relevant maturity determines management style: micromanage-level detail for someone new to a task is support; the same detail for a veteran is insulting — calibrate per person per task, not per person.

## Apex practices
- Run a weekly written "state of the team" review for yourself: delivery risks, people risks, morale signals — problems announce themselves months early in this log.
- Manage energy allocation deliberately: audit your calendar monthly against your stated priorities; the gap is your real strategy.
- Keep skin in the technical game via design reviews and reading PRs — not by taking critical-path coding tasks that make you the bottleneck.
- Write down your operating manual (how you decide, when to escalate to you, what you never want surprises about) and share it with the team in week one.

## Pitfalls
- Continuing to be the best engineer on the team — you starve seniors of growth and become a single point of failure.
- Shielding the team from all context "to protect focus," which produces engineers who make locally-optimal, globally-wrong decisions.
- Measuring activity (commits, tickets closed) instead of outcomes; Goodhart's law converts any surrogate metric into a target that gets gamed.

## Tools & references
