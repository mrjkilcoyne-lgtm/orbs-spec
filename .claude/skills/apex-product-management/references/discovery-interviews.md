# Discovery Interviews

## Scope
Learning what customers actually need through interviews: question craft, bias avoidance, synthesis, and connecting insight to product decisions.

## Core principles
- People are unreliable narrators of their future behavior but excellent narrators of their past: ask "walk me through the last time you…" instead of "would you use…" (the core of Fitzpatrick's "The Mom Test" — questions your mom couldn't lie to you about).
- Compliments are data-free: "that sounds cool, I'd totally buy it" is social lubricant; the only reliable signals are past behavior, current spend/workarounds, and concrete commitments (intro, deposit, calendar time).
- Interview for the struggle, not the feature: Jobs-to-be-Done framing (what progress were they trying to make, what did they fire to hire this?) surfaces the causal "why" that feature-request lists hide.
- Talk-to-listen ratio should be lopsided (~90% them); every time you explain your idea mid-interview you contaminate the remaining answers.
- Saturation, not sample size, ends discovery: after 5-8 interviews in a tight segment, new patterns stop appearing (Nielsen's small-n logic); 5 interviews across 5 different segments teaches almost nothing about any of them.

## Apex practices
- Run continuous discovery (Teresa Torres): a standing weekly cadence of customer touches by the product trio (PM + design + engineering), not a one-off "research phase" before the roadmap.
- Recruit on recency of the behavior ("did X in the last 30 days"), and screen out serial research participants and your own fans.
- Record and transcribe (with consent), then synthesize with interview snapshots or an opportunity solution tree — memory-based synthesis reliably remembers the quotes that confirm the plan.
- Separate the interview from the pitch: run problem interviews first; solution/prototype feedback is a different session with different rules (watch behavior on the prototype, ignore verbal praise).

## Pitfalls
- Leading questions with the answer embedded ("Don't you find deployments frustrating?") — the interviewee obliges, the transcript looks like validation.
- Pitching then "interviewing": once they know what you want to hear, every answer is a favor.
- Interviewing whoever is easiest (existing power users, friendly logos) and generalizing to the segment you actually need to win.

## Tools & references
Fitzpatrick "The Mom Test," Torres "Continuous Discovery Habits," Bob Moesta's JTBD interview method ("Demand-Side Sales"), Portigal "Interviewing Users," Dovetail/Grain for synthesis.
