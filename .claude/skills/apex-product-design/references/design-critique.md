# Design Critique

## Scope
Giving and receiving design feedback: critique culture, communication, constructive criticism, and improving as a team.

## Core principles
- Critique is feedback on the work, not the person — separate the idea from the designer to enable learning.
- Good critique asks "why" before "how" — understanding intent and constraints reveals whether to critique or support.
- Feedback should be specific (not "I like it") with rationale (not just opinions) — "This CTA is hard to find because it's the same size as secondary links" is useful; "I don't like the color" is not.
- Critique is a skill that improves with practice; junior designers should lead critiques, senior designers should ask questions to coach them.
- Psychological safety (fear of criticism kills honesty) — create norms that critique is expected and a sign of growth, not failure.

## Apex practices
- Structure critiques: context (what problem are you solving?), walkthrough (designer explains approach), feedback (team asks questions and offers specific critique).
- Defer implementation questions ("how do we build it") until after design is validated — early technical questions distract from design intent.
- Use questions to coach: "How would this work on mobile?" is more effective than "Make it mobile responsive."
- Document and close loops: write down feedback, prioritize it, iterate the design, and report back — otherwise feedback is forgotten.

## Pitfalls
- Critique that turns into brainstorming (50 ideas at once) instead of focused feedback on the proposed design.
- Dominant voices (senior people, extroverts) drowning out others — use silent feedback (Sticky notes, Figma comments) before verbal discussion.
- Deferring to authority ("The CEO likes it, so ship it") over user feedback — leadership input is one signal, not the only signal.

## Tools & references
Figma comments for asynchronous feedback, FigJam for collaborative critique, "Give and Get Good Design Feedback" (IDEO), psychological safety concepts from "Team of Teams" (McChrystal).
