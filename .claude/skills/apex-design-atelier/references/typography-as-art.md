# Typography as Art

## Scope
Letterforms as image: from calligraphy and Didot's rational letters through Futurist typography, Swiss modernism, and expressive digital type — making the text be the picture.

## Core principles
- Type has a voice before it has a meaning: a word set in Didone whispers luxury, in grotesque bold shouts protest, in Cooper Black grins — the reader hears the voice in the first 50ms, before reading. Choosing a face is casting an actor.
- The space is half the art: counters, kerning, leading, and margins are the negative-space composition; "typography is what happens between the letters." Tight display setting and airy text setting are different crafts with different rules.
- Two poles, both legitimate: crystal-goblet typography (Warde — invisible, serving the text) and typography-as-performance (Marinetti's explosions, Carson's grunge, kinetic type) — the sin is not choosing; body text pretending to perform, display type pretending to be invisible.
- Letterforms are drawings with 500 years of optics baked in: overshoots, optical centers, stroke modulation, ink traps — the eye's corrections encoded. Respect them when scaling/stretching (never distort type mechanically; use optical sizes and variable-font axes instead).
- Hierarchy is typographic before it is chromatic: size, weight, case, and spacing establish reading order on their own — a design that needs color to show structure has weak typographic bones.

## Apex practices
- Build the type system as a scale with jobs: display/heading/body/caption, each with size, leading, tracking, and measure (45-75 characters per line for body) — then compose pages using only the system.
- Learn to letter by hand occasionally: drawing an alphabet teaches stroke logic, spacing rhythm, and why fonts look the way they do — the Hockney daily-drawing principle applied to type.
- Exploit variable fonts as expressive axes: weight/width/optical-size animated or responsive — type that responds to context is the contemporary frontier of expressive typography.
- Pair faces by structural contrast with shared skeleton logic (a grotesque + a Didone; a humanist sans + its matching serif) and cap at two families per design until you can defend a third.

## Pitfalls
- Font tourism: six typefaces auditioning on one page — the type equivalent of the everything-brief.
- Squeezed/stretched/faux-bold type (mechanical distortion breaks the optical corrections).
- Display setting at body sizes: tight tracking, low contrast pairs, and hairline weights that dissolve below 20px.

## Tools & references
Bringhurst "The Elements of Typographic Style," "Thinking with Type" (Lupton), Fonts In Use, variable-fonts guides (web.dev), Typewolf for pairing taste.
