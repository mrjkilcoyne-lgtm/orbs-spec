# The Design Brief (Agency Craft)

## Scope
Writing and working from briefs the way the great agencies do — Ogilvy's research-driven salesmanship, Saatchi & Saatchi's brutal simplicity, Havas's meaningful-brands lens, Topham Guerin's speed-and-virality doctrine.

## Core principles
- One single-minded proposition: the brief must reduce to a sentence a stranger can repeat ("At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise is the electric clock"). Two ideas is zero ideas.
- Insight before idea (Ogilvy): "The consumer isn't a moron, she is your wife" — research the audience until the tension they feel is written in their own words; the creative leap starts there.
- The brief states the problem, never the solution: "make people feel the pension is theirs," not "use a piggy bank photo."
- Saatchi reduction: "Labour isn't working" is a poster, a policy argument, and a feeling in three words — cut until removing one more word breaks it.
- Speed is a creative property (Topham Guerin): shipping twenty rough executions into the live conversation beats one polished one after the moment has passed.

## Apex practices
- Write the brief with the classic skeleton: background → audience truth → single proposition → support → tone → mandatories → measure of success.
- Define tone as a spectrum with named poles ("more Attenborough than Clarkson") — adjectives alone ("bold, fresh") brief nothing.
- Pin the "moment of use": where and in what mood the audience meets the work decides format before aesthetics do.
- Pressure-test: if the proposition could appear on a competitor's brief unchanged, it isn't a proposition yet.

## Pitfalls
- The everything-brief: six objectives, four audiences, no decision made — the strategist's job was exported to the designer.
- Briefing execution details ("use our blue, big logo") while leaving the persuasion problem unstated.
- Confusing award-bait originality with effectiveness; the brief's success measure keeps the work honest.

## Tools & references
"Ogilvy on Advertising," "Confessions of an Advertising Man," D&AD annuals, the Saatchi poster canon, "How Brands Grow" (Sharp) for the evidence layer.
