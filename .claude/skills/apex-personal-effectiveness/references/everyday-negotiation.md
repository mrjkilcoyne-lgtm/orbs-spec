# Everyday Negotiation

## Scope
Getting to yes in high-stakes and routine conversations: salary, scope, deadlines, respect — structured approaches that preserve relationships while advocating for yourself.

## Core principles
- Negotiation is not win-lose; it's about understanding the other party's reservation price and discovering trades where you care more about X and they care more about Y (distributive vs. integrative).
- Information asymmetry decides most negotiations: entering with three competing offers, a strong BATNA (best alternative to negotiated agreement), or knowledge of market rates shifts the entire zone of possible agreement.
- Anchoring is real and first: the opening bid shapes the range of the negotiation by 15–20%, which is why saying "what's your first offer?" is a winning move.
- A collaborative framing ("I want to find something that works for both of us") changes the dynamic from adversarial to joint problem-solving — tone and language matter as much as numbers.
- Getting to no early (on a bad deal) is better than getting to a bad yes — learn to hear "no" without personalizing and to offer walk-away signals (different positions, not takebacks).

## Apex practices
- Before any negotiation, write your target, walk-away, and expected counteroffer; treat it like a meeting agenda so emotions don't hijack the math.
- Use silence strategically: after you make an offer, let them talk first — most people rush to fill silence and concede ground unnecessarily.
- Trade explicitly: "I can offer X if you move on Y" teaches the other party that movement is transactional, not a sign you'll bend further.
- Follow up in writing (email recap: "As we discussed, I understood you to say...") so expectations don't drift and you have a record.

## Pitfalls
- Internalizing rejection of an offer as personal rejection — it's a number, not a judgment; the other party's silence often means they're thinking, not refusing.
- Negotiating against yourself by improving your offer before they've countered — they don't know your reservation price unless you move, so let them move first.
- Confusing niceness with effectiveness; you can be warm and clear-eyed about your interests simultaneously.

## Tools & references
