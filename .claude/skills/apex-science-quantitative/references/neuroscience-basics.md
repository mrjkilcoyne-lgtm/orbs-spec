# Neuroscience Basics

## Scope
Brain structure and function: neurons, synapses, neurotransmitters, neural coding, learning, and the relationship between brain and behavior.

## Core principles
- Neurons (soma, dendrites, axon) integrate incoming signals; if membrane potential crosses threshold, action potential fires. Synapses (axon-terminal to dendrite) are the junctions where neurons communicate.
- Neurotransmitters (glutamate, GABA, dopamine, serotonin) are released presynaptically and bind postsynaptic receptors; they're excitatory or inhibitory, modulating firing probability.
- Neural coding: spike timing, rate (frequency), and population patterns encode information. Neurons in sensory cortex are selective (respond to specific features like orientation, color).
- Synaptic plasticity (long-term potentiation/depression) changes synapse strength based on activity; it's the cellular basis of learning. STDP (spike-timing-dependent plasticity) strengthens synapses if presynaptic fires before postsynaptic.
- The brain exhibits functional specialization (visual cortex for vision, motor cortex for movement) and hierarchical processing (early visual areas detect edges, higher areas recognize objects).

## Apex practices
- Use the concept of a receptive field: a neuron's preferred stimulus pattern and strength; understanding receptive fields guides experimental design.
- Remember that neural circuits are recurrent (feedback loops); recurrence enables working memory, gain modulation, and noise suppression.
- Recognize that brain activity (fMRI, EEG) reflects both synaptic input and spiking output; they're not identical and have different timescales.
- Apply evolutionary perspective: brain structures are conserved across species (mice, primates, humans) because core computations are similar; differences are often in circuit size/interconnection, not fundamental algorithm.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming one neuron = one feature; typically, a feature is encoded across a population (distributed coding).
- Confusing correlation (two neurons fire together) with causation (one drives the other); optogenetics or perturbation is needed for causation.
- Overgeneralizing from slice/cultured neurons to intact brains; context (neuromodulators, connectivity) dramatically shapes behavior.

## Tools & references
Kandel et al. "Principles of Neural Science," Dayan-Abbott "Theoretical Neuroscience," single-unit recording, whole-cell patch clamp, optogenetics.
