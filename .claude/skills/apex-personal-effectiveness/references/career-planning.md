# Career Planning

## Scope
Long-term trajectory across roles and organizations: identifying strengths, mapping opportunity, negotiating transitions, and building optionality for 10+ year horizons.

## Core principles
- Career capital (Duckworth, Ericsson): rare and valuable skills are built through years of deliberate practice in roles with feedback; they're the currency for choosing where to work and what to work on.
- The best time to negotiate was yesterday; the second best is now — salary, title, and domain expertise have exponential growth curves; 10% raises compound to 2.6x over a decade while staying put.
- Optionality is force-multiplication: before specializing deeply, keep 2–3 adjacent paths open by building skills that transfer; specialize only once the payoff is clear and the door isn't closing.
- The best career moves often feel like lateral moves — new role, same organization, or new organization, same skill — because you're shipping opportunity for skill depth or network leverage.
- Burnout signals a mismatch between your work and your values or energy reserves, not a personal failing; the solution is exit or reset, not toughness — talented people are always in demand elsewhere.

## Apex practices
- Map your 5-year target (role, skill depth, network position) and work backward: what job gets you there? What skills do you need in today's role? Which projects earn them?
- Invest 5% of work time outside your core job: take adjacent projects, present at conferences, maintain a portfolio or blog — it expands optionality and makes transitions faster.
- Build your network continuously, not during job search: monthly coffees with 2–3 peers, contribute to communities, share work publicly; weak ties (acquaintances) often bring the best opportunities.
- Negotiate compensation at every transition (job change, promotion, retention offer) because raises within a role regress toward the cost-of-living increase; each move is a multiplier event.

## Pitfalls
- Optimizing for title or company prestige instead of skill and network; a prestigious title with no visibility outside pays the same as an invisible job elsewhere.
- Staying in a comfortable, under-challenging role for 5+ years; atrophy of skills is real, and the re-entry cost compounds.
- Neglecting health, sleep, or relationships in pursuit of rank — burnout or regret later is not a tradeoff worth taking.

## Tools & references
Duckworth's "Grit," Newport's "So Good They Can't Ignore You," Herminia Ibarra's "Reinvent Yourself," annual 360 reviews or trusted peer feedback, salary transparency tools (Levels.fyi, Blind).
