# Market Sizing

## Scope
Estimating the addressable market (TAM, SAM, SOM) to determine whether a business can reach venture scale and to spot declining or expanding opportunities.

## Core principles
- Total addressable market (TAM) is the size of the entire market if you captured everyone; serviceable addressable market (SAM) is the part you can realistically reach; serviceable obtainable market (SOM) is what you'll actually capture in 3-5 years.
- Markets are not static; TAM can shrink (internal combustion engines), expand (smartphone market size 15 years ago vs. today), or shift (AWS cannibalized on-premise software TAM). Extrapolating from a shrinking TAM is a death trap.
- Top-down (market research, TAM reports) and bottom-up (customer count × ARPU) sizing should converge within a factor of 2-3; if they diverge, one assumption is wrong.
- Venture VC investors are looking for $1B+ TAM; smaller markets can support profitable businesses but not venture returns. A company in a $500M market needs 80%+ share to hit $400M revenue (unrealistic).
- Market sizing matters most during category creation; if you're entering an existing category (project management software), market size is known. If you're creating a category (no-code automation), TAM estimates are wildly uncertain and expand as category matures.

## Apex practices
- Build TAM from first principles (number of potential customers × average deal size) with transparent assumptions; a $10B TAM with 1% assumptions is more credible than a $100B TAM with no basis.
- Decompose TAM by geography and customer segment; enterprise software may have higher SAM in developed countries, SMB SaaS has global TAM.
- Track TAM expansion post-founding; Stripe's TAM expanded 10x as payments moved from desktop to mobile to embedded finance; founders who assume TAM is fixed leave upside on the table.
- Use proxy markets to size new categories; when estimating TAM for an AI-powered analytics tool, look at adjacent markets (BI software, analytics platforms) and estimate the addressable slice.

## Pitfalls
- Equating TAM with opportunity ("there's a $50B market") without clarifying whether you can reach it; TAM size matters, but competitor moat and distribution channel matter more.
- Using TAM to justify a high valuation without demonstrating a path to meaningful SAM; a $100B TAM is irrelevant if you can only capture 0.1% (SAM = $100M).
- Assuming TAM is static; companies that predicted digital transformation would shrink on-premise software were right, but missed how the market would expand in the cloud.
