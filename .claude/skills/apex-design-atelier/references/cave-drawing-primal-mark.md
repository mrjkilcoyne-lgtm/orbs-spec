# Cave Drawing & the Primal Mark

## Scope
Lascaux, Chauvet, Altamira: the first principles of image-making — economy of line, gesture as life, working with the surface — 30,000 years before design systems.

## Core principles
- Maximum life, minimum marks: a Chauvet horse is a dozen confident strokes that carry weight, motion, and species — proof that recognition needs gesture, not detail. The contour drawn from knowing the animal (not copying it) is the ancestor of every good icon and logo.
- The surface collaborates: painters used the rock's bulges as the animals' bellies and shoulders — the medium's accidents become the artwork's structure. Design corollary: work with the grain of the material (screen, paper, e-ink, terminal) instead of sanding it flat.
- Overlay without erasure: animals drawn across older animals for millennia — the wall is an accumulating conversation, not a replaced artifact. The first palimpsest, the first version history, the first collaborative canvas.
- Images are verbs: the paintings sit deep in the dark, hard to reach, likely part of ritual practice — made to do something (invoke, teach, bind a group), not to decorate. Ask of any image: what is it for? The answer shapes it more than aesthetics do.
- The toolkit was constraints: charcoal, ochre, iron oxide — three pigments, firelight, rock. The masterpieces argue that palette poverty concentrates rather than limits expression; every constraint-driven aesthetic since (ukiyo-e, risograph, 1-bit) rediscovers this.

## Apex practices
- Practice the contour drill: draw a known subject in ≤12 strokes, no correction — repeat until the strokes land where knowledge says, not where the eye wanders. This is icon design's core muscle.
- Before styling any surface, inventory its givens (texture, glare, refresh behavior, dead zones) and promote one given to a feature.
- Use the three-pigment exercise: design a full piece in charcoal-black, ochre-yellow, oxide-red on off-white — the original design tokens.
- Draw from memory after observation (look, close the reference, draw): memory strips detail and keeps structure — the abstraction machine that made the cave horses timeless.

## Pitfalls
- Confusing crude with primal: the cave lines are virtuosic, not naive — "primitive style" faked with shaky strokes misses the confidence that is the whole content.
- Detail-first drawing: starting at the eye and rendering outward instead of capturing the whole gesture in seconds first.
- Treating the surface as a neutral rectangle when it never is.

## Tools & references
Chauvet (Herzog's "Cave of Forgotten Dreams"), Lascaux archives, gesture-drawing practice (Nicolaïdes "The Natural Way to Draw"), Bradshaw Foundation rock-art library.
