# Storytelling

## Scope
Narrative structure that engages audiences: character, conflict, resolution, and emotional arcs; application to business, presentations, and content.

## Core principles
- Stories work because they engage emotion and memory; abstract arguments fade, but vivid narratives stick. The brain pattern-matches against stored stories.
- The classic arc (setup, conflict, climax, resolution) is not universal but powerful: introduce a relatable character, their obstacle, how they overcame it, and what changed.
- Stakes must be clear: why does this character's problem matter? Personal stakes (survival, love, identity) resonate more than abstract stakes (market share, efficiency).
- Specificity and sensory detail (sight, sound, smell) make stories vivid; "She felt the cold metal chair" beats "The chair was uncomfortable."
- Vulnerability and authenticity create trust; perfect heroes with no flaws or doubts bore audiences. Showing your struggle makes you credible.

## Apex practices
- Use the "hero's journey" framework (call to adventure, refusal, crossing threshold, tests, ordeal, reward) for longer narratives or presentations.
- Start in the middle (in medias res) to hook attention; exposition comes later. Open with conflict, not backstory.
- Use dialogue and specific examples rather than summary; show, don't tell. "The client left the meeting angry" beats "The client was unhappy."
- End with impact: what changed? What did the character (and by extension, the audience) learn? The resolution should echo the original stakes.

## Pitfalls
- Over-explaining; audiences resent being told what to feel or conclude. Trust them to draw meaning from the narrative.
- Protagonists with no flaws; relatable characters have weaknesses and grow. Perfection is boring.
- Disconnecting the story from the message; a story should illuminate your point, not overshadow it.

## Tools & references
McKee's "Story," Campbell's "The Hero with a Thousand Faces," Freytag's pyramid, TED talk structure (personal story → insight → universal principle).
