# Hiring & Interviewing

## Scope
Designing hiring processes that predict on-the-job performance: sourcing, structured interviews, debriefs, and closing.

## Core principles
- Unstructured interviews are among the worst predictors of job performance (Schmidt & Hunter meta-analysis, validity ~0.38 vs ~0.51 for structured); work samples and structured interviews dominate — gut feel is noise wearing a suit.
- Every interview must test a pre-declared signal with a pre-declared rubric; if two interviewers can watch the same candidate and score differently, you're measuring interviewer mood.
- Hiring is a funnel with compounding loss: a 10% improvement in top-of-funnel quality beats a 10% improvement in final-round accuracy — invest in sourcing and job-description precision first.
- False positives cost 10-20x more than false negatives (severance, morale, backfill time), but systematic false negatives quietly homogenize your team — track who you reject, not just who you hire.
- The candidate is interviewing you: every touchpoint (response latency, interviewer preparedness, offer speed) is evidence about what working there is like, and strong candidates read it fluently.

## Apex practices
- Use work-sample exercises that mirror the actual job (review this PR, debug this failing test, design this feature) rather than algorithm puzzles for roles that never invert binary trees.
- Run debriefs where interviewers submit written scores before hearing anyone else's opinion — anchoring by the most senior voice in the room is the classic debrief failure.
- Calibrate interviewers: shadow → reverse-shadow → solo, and periodically compare interviewer scores against 12-month performance of hires.
- Make offers fast (24-48h from final round) and have the hiring manager, not the recruiter, deliver the call.

## Pitfalls
- "Raising the bar" by adding rounds — each added round loses candidates to faster competitors while adding correlated, not independent, signal.
- Hiring for "culture fit" without a definition, which in practice means "reminds me of me"; define culture-add behaviors instead.
- Letting the loop test the same signal five times (five coding rounds, zero collaboration signal).

## Tools & references
Schmidt & Hunter (1998) selection-methods meta-analysis, "Who: The A Method for Hiring" (Smart & Street), structured rubrics in Greenhouse/Ashby, Laszlo Bock "Work Rules!"
