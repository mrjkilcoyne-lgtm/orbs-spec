# Risk Management

## Scope
Identifying, assessing, and mitigating business risks: financial risk, operational risk, strategic risk, and compliance risk.

## Core principles
- Risk has two dimensions: probability (how likely) and impact (how bad if it happens); prioritize risks that are both likely and high-impact, not just scary.
- Mitigation strategies are transfer (buy insurance), reduce (operational controls), avoid (don't do the risky thing), or accept (tolerate and reserve capital); most businesses use a mix.
- Black swan events (high impact, unexpected) are different from known risks; COVID was a black swan for restaurants but known risk for insurance companies. Hedging known risks is smart; hedging black swans is expensive (tail risk insurance).
- Concentration risk (customer, vendor, geography, revenue source) kills companies more than market risk; a company with 40% of revenue from one customer is at existential risk if that customer leaves.
- Financial risk (liquidity, solvency, interest rate) must be monitored; a company with 2 months of cash reserves and unpredictable revenue is vulnerable, while one with 12 months and stable revenue is resilient.

## Apex practices
- Maintain a risk register (identified risks, probability, impact, mitigation strategy, owner) and review quarterly; risks change as the company scales.
- Model financial stress scenarios (recession, key customer loss, key person departure) and discuss what would trigger each; knowing the trigger points means you can act before crisis.
- Diversify customer base (target max 20% from any single customer); diversify revenue streams (don't rely 100% on one product); diversify geography (not all revenue from one market).
- Build reserves for likely risks (bad debt, warranty claims) and insurance for catastrophic risks (liability, property damage, D&O insurance for boards).

## Pitfalls
- Ignoring low-probability, high-impact risks (complete data loss, key person death, major lawsuit); insurance or redundancy is cheap vs. the impact.
- Over-hedging known risks and creating opportunity cost; spending 5% of revenue to insure against 1% probability risks is often irrational.
- Treating risk as a CFO problem rather than a board and CEO problem; strategic risks (product obsolescence, competitive threat) require senior attention.

## Tools & references
ISO 31000 (risk management standard), McKinsey risk frameworks, actuarial tables (probability of claims), insurance brokers (specialized by industry), Deloitte risk assessment methodologies.
