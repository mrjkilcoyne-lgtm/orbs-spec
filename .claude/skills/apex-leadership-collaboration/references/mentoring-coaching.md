# Mentoring & Coaching

## Scope
Growing others' capability through advice-giving (mentoring) and question-asking (coaching), and knowing which mode the moment needs.

## Core principles
- Mentoring transfers your map ("here's what worked for me"); coaching helps them draw their own — and defaulting to mentoring when coaching is needed produces dependence, not growth.
- The GROW model (Goal, Reality, Options, Will) structures a coaching conversation so the coachee does the thinking; your job is questions like "what have you already tried?" not answers.
- Sponsorship beats mentorship for career outcomes: a mentor talks to you, a sponsor talks about you in rooms you're not in — allocating stretch projects and dropping names in calibration is where careers actually move.
- The advice trap (Bungay Stanier): your brain generates advice within seconds of hearing a problem, and it's usually advice for a problem they don't have — stay curious a little longer ("and what else?").
- Zone of proximal development (Vygotsky): growth happens on tasks just beyond current ability with support available; too easy teaches nothing, too hard teaches fear.

## Apex practices
- Open coaching conversations with "do you want my thoughts, or do you want to think out loud?" — the answer changes everything about the next 30 minutes.
- Debrief experiences, not just problems: after a launch or incident, ask "what would you do differently?" before offering your read — self-generated lessons stick, delivered lessons slide.
- As a mentor, share failures with the same specificity as successes; sanitized war stories teach that seniors never struggled, which is both false and demoralizing.
- Set an explicit arc for formal mentorship (goal, cadence, end date ~6 months); undefined mentorships decay into awkward recurring calendar debt.

## Pitfalls
- Solving the problem in the first five minutes and calling it coaching.
- Cloning: steering mentees toward your path and strengths instead of theirs — the point is their best self, not your understudy.
- Only mentoring people who remind you of you; sponsorship gaps, not skill gaps, are why underrepresented engineers stall — audit who gets your discretionary attention.

## Tools & references
Whitmore "Coaching for Performance" (GROW), Bungay Stanier "The Coaching Habit," Hewlett "Forget a Mentor, Find a Sponsor," Plato and Merit mentorship platforms.
