# Audio Production

## Scope
Capturing, mixing, and mastering sound: microphone technique, signal flow, compression, EQ, spatial effects, and critical listening in controlled environments.

## Core principles
- Sound is pressure waves; recording, mixing, and playback are transformations of those waves through equipment (transducers, gear, speakers), so understanding the signal path is non-negotiable.
- The acoustic environment (room reflections, background noise) is part of the recording; treating a room with absorption, bass traps, and diffusion transforms recording quality and mixing accuracy.
- Critical listening (on reference monitors in a treated space) is how you hear truth; consumer headphones, phone speakers, and untreated rooms lie about what's actually there.
- Compression (reducing loudness of loud parts) is the most important tool: it glues sources, controls dynamics, and shapes tone; it's not about "making it louder."
- Mixing is a hierarchy of priority: establish levels and panning, then EQ to fix frequency balance, then compression to shape, then effects for space — order matters because each affects perception of the next.

## Apex practices
- Record with the best microphone available for the source, placed strategically (proximity, angle, pops screen); mic technique is more important than preamp brand.
- Use a high-pass filter on every track that doesn't need low-end (vocals, guitar, cymbals); it removes rumble that muddies the mix without changing audible frequencies.
- EQ subtractive (removing problem frequencies) before additive (boosting) — boosting compounds, subtracting fixes.
- Use automation (volume, panning, effects) to evolve the mix; static mixing sounds static; dynamic mixing sounds produced.

## Pitfalls
- Mixing loud so you don't hear mistakes; lower levels reveal mix balance better (Haas effect, equal-loudness curves).
- Too much compression or reverb (assuming more is more); restraint is a skill.
- Ignoring the low-frequency domain (bass, kick) and wondering why the mix lacks power.

## Tools & references
Dante's "The Art of Mixing," Edmondson's "The Complete Modern Drummer," Critical listening: reference monitors (Genelec, Neumann), DAWs (Pro Tools, Ableton, Logic, Studio One), compression concepts (SSL, Neve models).
