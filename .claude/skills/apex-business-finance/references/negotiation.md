# Negotiation

## Scope
Commercial negotiation: contract terms, pricing, equity splits, employment, and vendor agreements where both parties must agree.

## Core principles
- Negotiation starts with information asymmetry; the party who knows the other's BATNA (best alternative to negotiated agreement) has leverage. Always understand your walk-away point and try to obscure it.
- Anchoring (the first number offered) disproportionately influences the final agreement; low anchors pull down settlements even if they're "unrealistic" — anchoring is powerful regardless of reasonableness.
- Creating additional value (expanding the pie) before claiming it (negotiating over slices) creates better outcomes; a land-and-expand deal might offer lower per-unit price in year 1 in exchange for a path to higher volume.
- Reciprocity and multiple simultaneous negotiations increase leverage; if you're negotiating a vendor contract and have a competing quote, that bid — even if not true — changes the negotiation.
- Written agreements prevent memory conflicts later; a handshake deal on vendor terms that shifts 6 months in creates disputes. Verbal agreements should be summarized in writing within 48 hours.

## Apex practices
- Prepare deeply: know your reservation price (walk-away price) and the other side's likely reservation price, and identify 3-5 dimensions to negotiate on (price, term length, payment schedule, exclusivity) beyond just price.
- Make the first move (anchor) and do so with reasoning; "we expect 2x pricing" is weak; "based on your current usage, you'll hit 1M queries annually, at $10/1M that's $10k," is strong because it's grounded in their data.
- Separate negotiation from the relationship; "this deal works for us only if we hit 20% margin" is less contentious than "you're being unreasonable."
- Document agreements in writing (email summary is fine for small deals) with specific terms (payment schedule, renewal terms, what "best effort" means, liability caps) — handshake is fine, handshake without summary is a trap.

## Pitfalls
- Negotiating the same terms repeatedly; if vendor pricing works for one customer, scale it and stop negotiating (transactional negotiation is a cost center).
- Using ultimatums early; ultimatums work only if you have leverage and are willing to walk away. If you don't walk away, credibility dies and future negotiations weaken.
- Anchoring too high (no seller will engage) or conflating reserve price with ask price; if your BATNA is $10, your ask should be $15-20, not $50.

## Tools & references
