# Rothko & Color Fields

## Scope
Rothko's stacked veils of color as emotional instrument — atmosphere, scale, and slowness as design materials.

## Core principles
- Rothko's subject was "basic human emotions — tragedy, ecstasy, doom"; color fields are the delivery mechanism, not the content. Design translation: choose the feeling first, then engineer the atmosphere that produces it.
- The glow comes from layering, not mixing: dozens of thin translucent veils, edges left soft and breathing — depth from superimposition. Digitally: layered translucent fills, soft gradients, blur — never one flat hex value pretending to be atmosphere.
- Scale is part of the medium: the canvases are large so they fill peripheral vision — you're in the color, not looking at it. Interfaces have the same lever: full-bleed immersion vs contained card change the emotional register entirely.
- Hovering, not resting: the rectangles float against their ground with ambiguous edges; the slight instability keeps the image alive. Hard edges declare; soft edges breathe — choose per emotional intent.
- Slowness is designed: Rothko wanted viewers at eighteen inches, dwelling. Some surfaces should reward the second minute, not the first glance — reading modes, meditative states, ambient dashboards.

## Apex practices
- Build color depth by stacking: 3-7 translucent layers of near-hues instead of one opaque fill; the vibration between layers is the "glow."
- Reduce the composition to 2-3 zones with one dominant: Rothko's format (large field, supporting band, breathing ground) is a usable emotional layout grammar.
- Prototype in the target light and size: color fields transform between phone and wall, dark room and daylight — judge at deployment scale (Rothko controlled gallery lighting for the same reason).
- Use temperature adjacency for mood: maroon-on-black hums differently than orange-on-yellow at identical layouts — run the palette as the variable while structure holds.

## Pitfalls
- Gradient-as-decoration: a purple blur behind a hero section is not a color field; without emotional intent and layered depth it's stock atmosphere.
- Text legibility sacrificed to the veil — Rothko had no body copy; you do.
- Ignoring ambient conditions (dark mode, night use) that invert the felt temperature of the palette.

## Tools & references
The Rothko Chapel, Seagram murals (Tate), "Mark Rothko: The Artist's Reality"; craft parallels: James Turrell's light rooms, "Interaction of Color" (Albers).
