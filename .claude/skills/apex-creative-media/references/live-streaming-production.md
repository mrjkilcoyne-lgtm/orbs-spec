# Live Streaming Production

## Scope
Broadcasting real-time content: camera work, transitions, graphics overlay, chat interaction, and managing production quality while live (no do-overs).

## Core principles
- Live streaming is television, not cinema: you have one chance to capture moments, so composition must be intentional, and preparation is non-negotiable (shot lists, graphics, transitions pre-tested).
- Bitrate and resolution are locked by platform constraints (Twitch: 1080p60 6Mbps max, YouTube: variable but recommend 8–15Mbps); know your platform's limits and gear accordingly.
- Audio quality matters more than video: viewers tolerate low-res video but abandon streams with bad audio; a mixer and monitoring setup is essential.
- Latency (delay between live moment and audience view) is typically 5–30 seconds; it breaks real-time interaction, so acknowledge it and don't try to respond to chat like it's instant.
- Multiple camera switching, graphics, and sound mixing require coordination (production workflow) or automation (OBS macros, hotkeys, presets) — ad-hoc switching looks amateur.

## Apex practices
- Rehearse transitions, graphics placement, and audio levels before going live; problems discovered live are broadcast problems.
- Use a mixer (dedicated audio interface or software mixer like Behringer) to separately control camera, game/desktop audio, and mic; it's faster than fiddling with system levels.
- Plan camera positions for depth and variety: wide shot of setup, close-up for detail, medium for presenter — cut every 5–10 seconds to maintain visual interest.
- Engage with chat selectively; constant interaction slows content delivery, but periodic acknowledgment builds community.

## Pitfalls
- Going live with untested graphics, transitions, or audio routing; the first stream is guaranteed chaos if you're discovering problems live.
- Ignoring audio monitoring; you can't hear yourself and become overly loud, quiet, or don't realize the mic is off.
- Poor camera positioning (too wide, from below, in bad light) that makes content hard to watch.

## Tools & references
OBS Studio (free, fully-featured), StreamDeck (hotkey controller), Streamlabs OBS, Twitch/YouTube studio UI, StreamYard (cloud-based multi-camera), audio interface (Focusrite, Behringer).
