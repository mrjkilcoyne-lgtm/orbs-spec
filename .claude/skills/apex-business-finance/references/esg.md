# ESG

## Scope
Environmental, social, and governance considerations in business: sustainability, diversity and inclusion, ethics, and stakeholder accountability.

## Core principles
- ESG is not purely altruistic; investors increasingly use ESG scores to assess long-term risk and resilience (climate risk, labor risk, governance risk). A B+ company on ESG metrics can face stranded asset risk (oil company valuations), while ESG leaders often outperform.
- Environmental risk varies by industry: a carbon-intensive manufacturer faces regulatory risk and customer pressure; a software company's environmental risk is primarily in datacenter energy consumption and e-waste.
- Social risk (labor practices, community impact, diversity) affects employee retention, customer loyalty, and regulatory relationships; companies with strong social practices have lower turnover and higher recruiting leverage.
- Governance risk (board independence, executive compensation, audit effectiveness) correlates with fraud risk and stock price stability; weak governance is material to long-term value.
- ESG reporting is increasingly mandatory for public companies (SEC, EU taxonomy) and requested by institutional investors; ESG standards (GRI, SASB, TCFD) create reporting frameworks.

## Apex practices
- Conduct a materiality assessment (which ESG factors matter most to your business and stakeholders) and focus on material issues; reporting on immaterial issues dilutes credibility.
- Set science-based targets for material issues (carbon neutrality by 2030, diversity targets) with third-party accountability; aspirational targets without measurement are greenwashing.
- Build ESG into incentive plans (executive compensation tied to ESG metrics) so leadership is accountable; ESG that doesn't affect incentives is theater.
- Engage with stakeholders (employees, customers, communities, investors) on ESG issues; internal diversity goals mean nothing if employees don't feel included, and sustainability claims mean nothing without supply chain transparency.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming ESG is cost-neutral or positive ROI; some ESG initiatives (renewable energy, carbon offsets) have upfront cost without immediate financial payoff.
- Confusing CSR (corporate social responsibility — charitable giving) with ESG (business risk management); ESG is about long-term value, not brand-washing.
- Setting ESG targets you can't measure or meet; materiality in goal-setting matters (80% of employees from underrepresented groups is unrealistic if the market is 20%; target 20%+ of hires).

## Tools & references
GRI (Global Reporting Initiative) standards, SASB (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board) by industry, TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures), MSCI ESG ratings, Bloomberg ESG data, Equileap (gender equality metrics).
