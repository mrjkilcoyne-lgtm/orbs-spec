# Scientific Method

## Scope
The systematic process of inquiry: observation, hypothesis formation, prediction, experimentation, and revision based on evidence.

## Core principles
- Reproducibility is the foundation: independent researchers should be able to repeat an experiment with the same procedure and get consistent results. Publication, materials, code, and data must be sufficiently detailed.
- Falsifiability (Popper): a hypothesis is scientific if it can be proven false; unfalsifiable claims (e.g., "a hidden force explained everything") are dogma, not science.
- Null hypothesis significance testing (NHST) is one framework; it has limitations (p-values are misinterpreted). Bayesian, information-theoretic, and replication-focused alternatives exist.
- Measurement error and confounding are ever-present; controls (sham treatments, negative controls) and blinding prevent experimenter bias.
- Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence; priors on implausible hypotheses should be high, requiring more data to overturn them.

## Apex practices
- Pre-register hypotheses, analysis plans, and sample sizes before data collection; this prevents p-hacking and multiple-comparisons bias.
- Report effect sizes, confidence intervals, and raw data (or sufficient statistics); p-values alone are insufficient for scientific inference.
- Distinguish exploratory (hypothesis-generating) from confirmatory (hypothesis-testing) research; label them clearly so readers understand the stage.
- Replicate important findings with new samples and slightly different methods; single studies are suggestive, repeated successes are convincing.

## Pitfalls
- Publication bias and selective reporting: studies with surprising results or p<0.05 get published; failures to replicate sit in file drawers.
- HARKing (Hypothesizing After Results are Known): running analyses first, then claiming predictions; pre-registration fixes this.
- Confirmation bias: interpreting ambiguous results as supporting your hypothesis; seek disconfirming evidence actively.

## Tools & references
Popper "The Logic of Scientific Discovery," Kuhn "The Structure of Scientific Revolutions," OSF (Open Science Framework) for pre-registration, Cochrane reviews for evidence synthesis.
