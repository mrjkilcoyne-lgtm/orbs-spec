# Video Editing

## Scope
Assembling, trimming, and pacing footage: cut sequences, pacing rhythm, transitions, color correction, and building narrative or energy through editorial choices.

## Core principles
- The cut is the fundamental tool: how close shots are timed relative to dialogue, music, or action determines pacing and emotional impact; faster cuts create energy, longer shots allow breathing.
- Continuity (matching action, eyeline, axis) makes invisible editing; violating continuity deliberately creates dissonance (jump cuts, match cuts) that should be intentional, not accidental.
- The sound design is half the experience; dialogue, music, and effects are edited and mixed with the same care as picture; weak sound makes a good picture feel cheap.
- Montage is compression and juxtaposition: a series of quick cuts to music can convey passage of time or emotional journey in seconds; pacing is controlled by cut rhythm.
- Color correction is technical (color grading) and aesthetic; different shots need to match in tone and color so they feel cohesive, especially in dynamic scenes.

## Apex practices
- Rough cut first with all dialogue/narration, then add music (or create a temp track), then refine picture edit against the sound; sound and picture must marry.
- Use J-cuts (sound starts before picture change) and L-cuts (picture changes before sound ends) to smooth transitions and create flow.
- Organize clips (bins by scene, by type) and use markers (dialogue, action, music cues) so you can navigate quickly and find sync points.
- Conform to color and audio standards (codec, bit depth, sample rate) before export; many "broken" deliverables are encoding issues, not creative ones.

## Pitfalls
- Inserting every piece of footage because it's cool; restraint and choosing the best takes makes pacing tight.
- Forgetting to account for audio (music, SFX) when timing picture cuts — picture and sound must have compatible pacing.
- Exporting in wrong format/codec and losing quality or compatibility.

## Tools & references
Premiere Pro (industry standard, flexible), Final Cut Pro (fast rendering, optimized), DaVinci Resolve (color grading strength, free tier), Avid (broadcast standard), Bordwell's "The Way Hollywood Tells It" (cutting theory).
