# Composition Rules

## Scope
The load-bearing geometry of images and layouts: grids, hierarchy, negative space, rhythm, and the eye's actual path.

## Core principles
- Hierarchy is the first deliverable: a composition is an argument about what matters most; if two elements compete for first, the composition has failed before style begins. One dominant, few subdominants, everything else subordinate.
- The eye follows gradients of contrast: size, value, color, isolation, and faces/text pull attention in roughly that order — you are always designing an eye-path, whether you meant to or not.
- Negative space is structural: the empty areas define the full ones (figure/ground); crowding is the most common amateur tell, and generous margins are the cheapest signal of confidence in the content.
- Grids create rhythm, alignment creates trust: near-alignment reads as error; either align or make the offset unmistakably deliberate. Break the grid exactly where you want the accent — a break is a spotlight, so spend it once.
- Balance is weight physics: every element has visual mass (size × value contrast × complexity); compositions balance masses around a fulcrum — symmetric (formal, static) or asymmetric (dynamic, modern), but never accidental.

## Apex practices
- Thumbnail before executing: 6 postage-stamp studies of mass arrangement in 5 minutes beat 2 hours of polishing the first idea — composition is decided at blob scale.
- Squint (or blur) test every layout: at 10% detail only hierarchy and balance survive; if the blur doesn't show your intended reading order, no amount of styling will.
- Use interval deliberately: equal spacing = calm inventory; progressive spacing = motion; one broken interval = accent. Spacing is the composer's timing.
- Check the edges and corners: the frame is part of the composition — elements kissing the edge create tension; know each tangent and mean it.

## Pitfalls
- Centering everything (the beginner's symmetric graveyard) or scattering everything (fear of commitment) — both refuse the hierarchy decision.
- Filling all space because it's there; horror vacui strangles emphasis.
- Rules-of-thirds cargo culting: the rule is a training-wheels approximation of asymmetric balance, not a law — placing things on intersections mechanically produces mechanical images.

## Tools & references
"Notan" studies, Müller-Brockmann "Grid Systems in Graphic Design," "Picture This" (Molly Bang — the best composition book disguised as a children's book), Edward Tufte on data-ink for the analytical case.
