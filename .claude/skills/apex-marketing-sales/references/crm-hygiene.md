# CRM Hygiene

## Scope
Maintaining accurate, actionable customer relationship management data: data entry discipline, field standardization, lead scoring, and usage policies.

## Core principles
- A CRM is only useful if it's accurate; garbage in, garbage out. A CRM with 50% stale data creates noise that drowns out signal.
- Standardization (mandatory fields, field definitions, drop-down options) enables reporting and forecasting; "some deals are in 'proposal,' some in 'under review,' some in 'waiting for finance'" makes reporting impossible.
- Lead scoring (automatic ranking by conversion likelihood) helps reps prioritize; a simple rule (company size × engagement level × budget signal) surfaces the most viable prospects.
- Data governance (who enters data, when, what's required) must be light enough to not feel like busy-work but strict enough to maintain quality.
- CRM usage is cultural; if leadership doesn't use the CRM, neither will the team. Leading by example (updating your own deals, using CRM reports in meetings) signals importance.

## Apex practices
- Implement automated data enrichment (tools like RocketReach, Apollo, ZoomInfo pre-fill data from public sources) to reduce manual entry and improve accuracy.
- Create mandatory fields that unlock deals (can't close a deal without customer size, can't move past discovery without decision maker identified); this forces discipline early.
- Build dashboards for sales ops and sales leadership (pipeline by rep, close rate by stage, sales cycle by customer size) and use them in weekly reviews.
- Audit CRM data monthly (are deals stalled for months? are old opportunities never closed? are mandatory fields missing?); identify patterns and coach.

## Pitfalls
- Requiring so many CRM fields that reps stop updating it; strike a balance between signal and friction.
- Using CRM as a surveillance tool (tracking reps' activity) instead of an enablement tool; this destroys trust and rep adoption.
- Failing to define field standards (close date, deal stage, customer segment) and allowing reps to use their own terminology; this makes company-level reporting impossible.
