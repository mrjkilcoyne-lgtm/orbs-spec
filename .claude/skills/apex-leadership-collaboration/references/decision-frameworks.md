# Decision Frameworks

## Scope
Structured approaches to making high-stakes decisions: reversible vs. irreversible, decision velocity, input gathering, and buy-in.

## Core principles
- Reversible and irreversible decisions require different speeds: fast iteration on reversible choices (Type 2 in Bezos terms), deep analysis and consensus on irreversible ones (Type 1).
- Decision quality depends on the frame: collect 3-5 divergent frames for high-stakes calls, not 10 options within the same frame.
- Separating "who decides" from "who advises" avoids consensus paralysis; state it explicitly: Does this need full alignment, delegation with input, or solo ownership?
- Decisions made under time pressure without documented rationale become invisible later — capture the tradeoff, not just the choice.
- Late discovery of a fatal flaw is cheaper than early discovery buried in a 50-page doc nobody read; use decision trees or pre-mortems to surface deal-breakers.

## Apex practices
- Build explicit decision templates for recurring classes (tech choices, hires, resource allocation) with fields for frames, options, constraints, and reversibility.
- Use pre-mortems: assume a decision failed catastrophically, work backward to likely causes, surface unknowns before locking in.
- When you have low information but time pressure, "fast reversible" beats "slow correct"; reframe as an 80/20 bet with a kill criterion.
- Document the "why" alongside the "what": future you (or a successor) will ask why that choice stuck when conditions changed.

## Pitfalls
- Seeking consensus on irreversible decisions (strategy, major hires, architectural bets); this invites design-by-committee.
- Reversing a decision publicly without explaining why shifts happened, eroding trust that decisions are principled.
- Collecting exhaustive analysis for decisions where 60% confidence suffices; kills velocity and postpones learning.

## Tools & references
Bezos Type 1/2 decisions, pre-mortem technique (Kahneman), Cynefin framework for decision complexity, decision matrices for structured trade-offs.
