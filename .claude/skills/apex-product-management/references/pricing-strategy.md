# Pricing Strategy

## Scope
Setting and structuring price for products: value metrics, packaging, price levels, and the research to inform them.

## Core principles
- Price is the exchange rate on value, and it has three anchors: cost-plus (floor), competitor-based (market context), and value-based (ceiling) — durable pricing works backward from quantified customer value, with the other two as sanity checks.
- The value metric (what you charge per: seat, usage, revenue share, outcome) matters more than the price level; a good value metric scales price with the value received (Twilio per message, Stripe per transaction) so expansion is built-in and small customers aren't priced out.
- Packaging is segmentation made concrete: Good-Better-Best tiers exploit willingness-to-pay differences across segments, and the middle tier is where design effort belongs (decoy/anchoring effects push buyers there — see "center-stage effect").
- Pricing is the strongest profit lever: at typical SaaS margins, a 1% price improvement lifts operating profit several times more than a 1% volume increase (McKinsey's classic waterfall analysis); it deserves quarterly attention, not once-at-launch.
- Willingness to pay can be measured, not guessed: Van Westendorp price-sensitivity meter for ranges, Gabor-Granger for demand curves at price points, conjoint analysis for feature-price trade-offs — 15-30 customer conversations with these beat any internal debate.

## Apex practices
- Re-price or re-package roughly annually: monetization work (Simon-Kucher / ProfitWell data) shows most SaaS companies are underpriced early, and grandfather clauses can de-risk increases for the installed base.
- Design tiers around customer segments and their jobs, not around feature lists ("Starter for solo founders, Growth for teams with compliance needs") — then fence with the 1-2 features each segment can't live without (SSO, audit logs, API limits).
- Test pricing with real commitment: proposals to real prospects at different price points (sales-led) or geo/cohort experiments (self-serve) — surveys alone overstate willingness to pay.
- Publish pricing when selling to SMB/mid-market self-serve motions; hide it only when deals are genuinely bespoke — "contact us" on a $99-shaped product suppresses the funnel.

## Pitfalls
- Cost-plus pricing for software, anchoring a 10x-value product to marginal cost near zero, then wondering why enterprise buyers doubt its seriousness.
- One-size pricing: a single plan simultaneously overcharges the long tail and undercharges the whale who'd happily pay 10x for SSO and support SLAs.
- Discounting as the default closing tool — uncontrolled 30-40% end-of-quarter discounts train buyers to wait and destroy price integrity across renewals.

## Tools & references
Simon-Kucher literature, Madhavan Ramanujam "Monetizing Innovation," Van Westendorp / Gabor-Granger / conjoint methods, ProfitWell (Paddle) benchmarks, Kyle Poyar (OpenView) usage-based pricing guides.
