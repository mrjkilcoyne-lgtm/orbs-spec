# UX Research

## Scope
Understanding users through research: interview methods, surveys, testing, analysis, and turning insights into design direction.

## Core principles
- Qualitative research (interviews, observations) reveals "why" users act; quantitative (surveys, analytics) reveals "what" — both are essential, not alternatives.
- Recruiting for research should be diverse (age, context, ability, goal) — homogeneous samples only validate existing assumptions.
- Open-ended questions ("Tell me about...") generate insights; leading questions ("Do you like X?") confirm bias — listen more than you suggest.
- Sample size for interviews is 5–8 per segment; after that, findings repeat — diminishing returns on time investment.
- Triangulation (combining multiple methods: interviews + observation + diary studies) validates findings across contexts.

## Apex practices
- Create a research plan before recruiting: define research question, participant criteria, methodology, analysis approach — ad-hoc research wastes time.
- Record sessions (with permission) so you can review, quote, and revisit details later — live notes miss nuance.
- Analyze by coding themes: read transcripts, highlight quotes, group into themes — systematic, not cherry-picked anecdotes.
- Synthesize findings into personas or job-stories that the team can use; detached research reports are ignored.

## Pitfalls
- Interviewing only power users (existing customers) and missing non-users and churned users who have different needs.
- Confirming bias by asking leading questions or interpreting answers to match predictions.
- Research theater: running research without acting on findings — design something different anyway because stakeholder intuition "wins."

## Tools & references
Interviewing methods (Jobs to be Done interviews, user interviews), UserTesting (remote testing), Maze (unmoderated testing), Dovetail (analysis), affinity mapping; "Rocket Surgery Made Easy" (Krug); ethnographic research methods.
