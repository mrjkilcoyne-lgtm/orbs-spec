# Cap Tables

## Scope
Tracking and managing company ownership: equity allocation, dilution, option pools, and ensuring the cap table is audit-clean.

## Core principles
- A cap table is a detailed record of all equity (common stock, options, warrants, preferred stock) and who owns it; the fully diluted cap table includes the impact of option pool, all convertibles, and future funding dilution.
- Fully diluted ownership is the only thing that matters to employees and investors; if you own 5% pre-money and the company raises at 2x pre-money valuation with 50% option pool, your fully diluted ownership is roughly 2.5% (diluted by new investors and option pool).
- Option pool size (typically 10-15% for early-stage, 5-10% for Series B+) is a negotiation: VCs want it small (less dilution), employees want it large (more liquidity), but if it's too small, you'll have to refresh and dilute everyone again.
- Vesting (4-year cliff with 1-year cliff is standard) exists to protect the company against departing founders; acceleration on acquisition (single-trigger or double-trigger) protects employees against acquirers who don't want to assume equity obligations.
- Anti-dilution clauses (broad-based weighted average vs. narrow-based weighted average vs. full-ratchet) adjust option exercise prices when new rounds are raised at lower valuations; full-ratchet is aggressive and rarely used, broad-based weighted average is the market standard.

## Apex practices
- Use a cap table management tool (Carta, Pulley, Shareworks) from day one; a spreadsheet cap table at Series C is a nightmare for investors and auditors.
- At each funding round, recalculate fully diluted ownership for all shareholders; publish it (or at least inform key employees) so there are no surprises.
- Refresh option pools at Series A and B; if you started with a 10% option pool at inception and have hired 50 people, you need to grant more options. The refresh dilutes all shareholders equally.
- Maintain clean equity documentation: stock option agreements signed by employees, board resolutions authorizing issuance, and section 83(b) elections for founders (if not filed within 30 days of grant, options are taxed as ordinary income later).

## Pitfalls
- Starting without a vesting schedule (or with a vesting schedule not tied to time); founding equity should cliff at 1 year and vest over 4 years.
- Allowing common stock and preferred stock to have equal voting rights; preferred stock (held by VCs) typically has protective provisions (veto on salary expenses, acquisitions, major contracts) separate from voting rights.
- Failing to adjust the option pool when you've used it up; you'll announce new hire grants that don't exist (cap table is already fully diluted) or you'll issue options at random strike prices.

## Tools & references
Carta (the standard), Pulley, Shareworks, Model cap table (SeedLegals), Fenwick & West equity agreements, NCEO (National Center for Employee Ownership) guides, Cooley cap table template.
