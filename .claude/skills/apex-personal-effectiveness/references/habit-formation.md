# Habit Formation

## Scope
Building automatic behaviors and dismantling unwanted ones: cue-routine-reward loops, environment design, and habit tracking.

## Core principles
- Habits are context-triggered: the cue-craving-response-reward loop (Duhigg/Clear synthesis of Skinner) means the cue, not willpower, initiates behavior — change the cue and you change the behavior.
- Automaticity takes 18–254 days, median ~66 (Lally et al., 2010) — the "21 days" figure is folklore; complex habits take months, and missing one day measurably doesn't derail formation.
- Friction is destiny: 20 seconds of added friction (Achor) reliably suppresses a behavior, and 20 seconds removed promotes it — environment design beats motivation because it works when motivation is absent.
- Habit stacking exploits existing automaticity: anchoring a new behavior to an established one ("after I pour coffee, I write one sentence") borrows the old habit's cue reliability.
- Identity drives durability (Clear): "I'm a runner" sustains behavior that "I want to run more" cannot — each repetition is a vote for the identity, and the identity then protects the repetition.

## Apex practices
- Start below the failure threshold: Fogg's Tiny Habits — two minutes or two reps, so the habit is established before it is enlarged; scale intensity only after the trigger is automatic.
- Design the environment first: put the running shoes by the door, delete the app, use website blockers — one-time friction changes outperform daily willpower expenditure.
- Track with a visible chain (calendar X's) and adopt the "never miss twice" rule — one miss is an accident, two is the start of a new habit.
- To break a habit, keep the cue and reward but make the routine impossible or unattractive: same craving, substituted response (the core of habit-reversal training).

## Pitfalls
- Betting on motivation: motivation follows action more than it precedes it; systems that require feeling like it fail on the days that matter.
- Stacking five new habits simultaneously — attentional budget for habit formation is roughly one keystone habit at a time.
- All-or-nothing collapse: treating a single lapse as evidence of failure (the abstinence violation effect) and abandoning the streak entirely.

## Tools & references
Clear's "Atomic Habits," Fogg's "Tiny Habits," Duhigg's "The Power of Habit," Lally et al. (2010) automaticity study, Habitica/Streaks/paper calendar chains.
