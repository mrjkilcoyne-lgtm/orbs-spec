# Ergonomics & Health

## Scope
Physical setup and habits for knowledge workers: workstation ergonomics, movement, vision, and repetitive-strain prevention.

## Core principles
- Posture is dynamic, not positional: "the best posture is the next posture" — no static position is safe for 8 hours; variability (sit/stand cycling, micro-breaks) beats any single "correct" alignment.
- Neutral joint positions are the load-bearing rule: elbows ~90–110°, wrists straight (not extended on a raised keyboard), monitor top at or slightly below eye level about an arm's length away — every deviation is a repeated micro-load that compounds over years.
- RSI is a dose problem: force x repetition x duration x awkward posture; symptoms lag damage, so tingling or night pain is a late signal demanding immediate dose reduction, not a nuisance to push through.
- Sedentary time is an independent risk factor: prolonged sitting raises cardiometabolic risk even in people who exercise (Ekelund's meta-analyses suggest ~30–40 min of moderate activity/day offsets it, but breaking up sitting matters separately).
- Eyes need distance and blink: screen work halves blink rate and holds accommodation at fixed focus; the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, 20 seconds at 20 feet) plus deliberate blinking addresses the two mechanisms of digital eye strain.

## Apex practices
- Set the chair first (feet flat, thighs parallel, lumbar supported), then bring keyboard/mouse to the elbows, then the monitor to the eyes — in that order; most people arrange the monitor first and contort everything else to it.
- Use a break cadence tool (e.g., a 30–45 min movement reminder) and treat calls as walking opportunities; accumulate position changes rather than relying on one heroic standing block.
- Learn keyboard shortcuts and consider a split/tented keyboard and vertical mouse at the first sign of wrist symptoms — pointer-heavy workflows are the dominant RSI dose for most developers and designers.
- Anchor the non-negotiables: 150 min/week moderate cardio plus 2 resistance sessions (WHO guidelines), 7–9 hours sleep, and strength work for the posterior chain — desk symptoms are often weakness, not just posture.

## Pitfalls
- Buying a $1,500 chair or standing desk and using it in one fixed position all day — equipment without behavior change relocates the problem.
- Laptop-only work: the screen and keyboard cannot both be in neutral position on a laptop; without an external keyboard or riser, one joint system always loses.
- Ignoring early RSI signals and "stretching through" nerve symptoms — tendon issues and nerve entrapment (carpal/cubital tunnel) need load management first; stretching an inflamed nerve worsens it.

## Tools & references
OSHA/NIOSH workstation guidelines, Cornell University Ergonomics (CUErgo), "Deskbound" (Starrett), WHO physical-activity guidelines, break tools (Stretchly, Time Out), split keyboards/vertical mice.
