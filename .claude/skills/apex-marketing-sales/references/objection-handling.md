# Objection Handling

## Scope
Responding to buyer resistance: distinguishing objections from excuses, handling price objections, and negotiating without discounting.

## Core principles
- Objections are signals, not rejection; "we need to evaluate three vendors" is feedback that you're in the process but not the default. Silence is worse than objections.
- Price objections often mask deeper issues (you haven't built enough value, or you're talking to someone without authority); cutting price without fixing the underlying issue is a waste.
- Objections from low-authority participants (implementers, influencers) are often concerns, not showstoppers; objections from economic buyers are decisions. Qualify the objector.
- Common objections (we're using a competitor, we can't afford it, we need consensus, we need a trial) should have pre-built responses; surprises mean you're unprepared.
- The best objection is one you prevent; good discovery uncovers objections early (budget constraints, competitor commitments) when they're easiest to address.

## Apex practices
- Reframe objections as concerns to address, not barriers to overcome; "I understand why that's concerning, here's how other customers handled it" beats defensive arguing.
- Use the feel-felt-found framework: "I understand how you feel; other customers felt the same; what they found is [solution]." This validates concerns while moving forward.
- Never discount as the first response to "it's too expensive"; ask "what would make this a no-brainer for you?" and explore creative solutions (longer implementation, phased rollout, usage-based pricing).
- Document objections by deal and use them to coach team; if 50% of proposals hit the same objection, the sales messaging needs updating, not each rep needs a better answer.

## Pitfalls
- Being overly agreeable ("you're right, it is expensive"); agreement without solution ends the conversation.
- Discounting immediately; this trains buyers to expect price reductions and destroys future pricing power.
- Treating all objections as must-win; if an objection can't be overcome (they need on-premise only and you're SaaS), end the deal early.

## Tools & references
