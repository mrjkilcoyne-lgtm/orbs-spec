# Developer Relations

## Scope
Building community and trust with developer audiences: authentic engagement, educational content, and advocacy.

## Core principles
- Developers are skeptical; they value honesty about limitations and authentic engagement over marketing spin. Treat them as peers, not targets.
- Educational content (tutorials, talks, open-source contributions) builds goodwill and credibility faster than sales. Developers spread products they love.
- Feedback loops matter: listen to what developers say (on forums, GitHub issues, social media) and act on it. If you ignore problems they highlight, trust erodes.
- Developer experience (DX) is product experience for devs: clear docs, quick setup, and good error messages drive adoption. A frustrating API kills a good company.
- Community is a long-term play; supporting developers building on your platform (hackathons, dev grants, feature requests) compounds returns.

## Apex practices
- Create and showcase successful developer projects; a GitHub repo with good examples is better marketing than any white paper.
- Be transparent about roadmaps, limitations, and mistakes; developers respect honesty and punish hiding problems.
- Engage in public forums (Stack Overflow, Reddit, your community Slack) as yourself, not a corporate account; real humans build trust.
- Measure engagement metrics beyond downloads: what's the retention rate? How many developers are building production systems? Are they referring others?

## Pitfalls
- Launching a product without engaging developers early; feedback after launch is too late.
- Treating developer relations as marketing; it's a product and community function. Support, product management, and advocacy are inseparable.
- Overstating features or capabilities to close deals; developer trust is fragile and damaged quickly.

## Tools & references
GitHub discussions / Discord communities (engagement platforms), developer advocacy (Maya Kaczorowski, Amy Kamarainen), metrics (DAU, retention, NPS).
