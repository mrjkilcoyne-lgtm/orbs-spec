# HR Tech

## Scope
Human resources systems: recruitment, onboarding, payroll, benefits, performance management, and compliance.

## Core principles
- Payroll is complex and regulatory: tax withholding (federal, state, local, FICA), benefits deductions, garnishments, and timely payment are legal requirements; errors cascade to IRS and employee harm.
- Recruiting is asymmetric: companies have incentives to hire quickly and cheaply; candidates want fair, transparent processes; bias (unconscious discrimination, bad-actor pattern matching) must be actively prevented.
- Employee data is sensitive: health information (health insurance, disability), financial (salary, benefits), and personal; data handling is regulated (HIPAA, state privacy laws, company policies).
- Compliance varies by jurisdiction: federal (ADA, FMLA, EEOC), state (wage and hour, discrimination, family leave), and local (minimum wage, benefit mandates) laws are complex and change frequently.
- Retention is cheaper than recruiting: keeping employees costs less than hiring and training replacements; HR systems should surface flight risk and engagement issues.

## Apex practices
- Integrate with payroll systems (ADP, Gusto, Workday) for single source of truth on compensation; spreadsheet payroll is a liability.
- Implement skills/competency tracking: match employees to projects based on skills, identify training needs, and enable career growth paths.
- Use structured interviews and rubrics to reduce bias: unstructured conversations are inconsistent and prone to unconscious bias; standardized questions and scorings are more fair and defensible.
- Maintain audit trails for compliance: performance ratings, termination decisions, and discrimination complaints need documentation in case of legal challenge.

## Pitfalls
- Using proxy variables that encode discrimination: educational pedigree (weeds out non-traditional candidates), zip codes (weeds out minorities), or age heuristics (legal age discrimination).
- Misclassifying employees as contractors to avoid benefits/taxes; IRS has criteria, and misclassification has penalties.
- Ignoring mental health and burnout; high turnover signals culture problems, not hiring problems.

## Tools & references
Workday (enterprise HRIS), BambooHR (mid-market), Gusto (payroll, SMB), LinkedIn Recruiter, Indeed, Greenhouse (ATS), Glassdoor (employer branding), EEOC guidelines, wage/hour laws.
