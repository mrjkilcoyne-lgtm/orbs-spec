# Culture Building

## Scope
Shaping team norms, values, and behavior patterns: what decisions stick, who gets rewarded, how conflicts surface, the psychological safety baseline.

## Core principles
- Culture is not declared; it's lived. The stories your team tells about who got fired, promoted, or sidelined are more powerful than a mission statement.
- Norms are enforced at the boundaries: reward the behavior you want (delegate to junior staff, admit mistakes, say "I don't know") and discourage the opposite (solo heroics, hiding failures, politics).
- Psychological safety—where people speak up without fear—is the precondition for learning; measuring it (Edmondson surveys) is easier than building it but both matter.
- Onboarding is culture transfer: new hires absorb norms fastest in their first 90 days; design that period intentionally.
- Culture fragments under pressure; crisis reveals true values — if you claim "people over metrics" but ship layoffs by surprise, the gap is exposed instantly.

## Apex practices
- Articulate 3-5 core values as "and/and" statements that admit tradeoff (e.g., "Bias for action AND thorough thinking" not just "move fast").
- Operationalize values in hiring: interview for them explicitly, have examples, score the dimension alongside skill.
- Make the invisible visible: capture stories (written or video) of people exemplifying values and share in all-hands, team lunches, or onboarding.
- Set norms through ritual: when a leader admits a mistake publicly or a PM says "I was wrong," the team copies. Be the example.

## Pitfalls
- Espoused values that don't match enacted values (we say "empower" but punish failed experiments); this kills trust faster than no values at all.
- Scaling culture without intentional transfer: assuming new hires catch the vibe; instead, systematize stories, habits, and decision rules.
- Expecting culture to survive massive stress (layoffs, pivots) without explicit reinforcement; it doesn't.

## Tools & references
Edmondson's "Teaming" (psychological safety framework), Schein's "Organizational Culture and Leadership," Patty McCord's "Powerful," interview scorecards, ritual design.
