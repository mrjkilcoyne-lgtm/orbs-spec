# Roadmapping

## Scope
Communicating sequenced product intent over time: what problems will be tackled when, at the right altitude for each audience.

## Core principles
- A roadmap is a prototype of your strategy, not a release plan (Janna Bastow); the artifact's job is to force sequencing conversations, and it's wrong the moment it's printed.
- Commit to problems and outcomes, not features and dates — "reduce onboarding drop-off from 60% to 40% in Q3" survives learning; "ship wizard v2 on Sept 15" dies on first contact with discovery.
- Confidence decays with horizon: use Now/Next/Later (or quarter-level granularity that widens over time) rather than a 12-month Gantt chart that pretends December is as knowable as next sprint.
- Different audiences need different roadmaps: sales needs committed dates for contract-blocking items, execs need theme-level bets, the team needs outcome targets — one artifact serving all three serves none.
- Capacity is the silent variable: a roadmap that ignores KTLO/support load (typically 20-40% of engineering capacity) is fiction with milestones.

## Apex practices
- Run roadmap reviews as trade-off sessions: every addition names its displacement ("this enters, what exits?"), which converts stakeholder lobbying into prioritization.
- Annotate items with confidence levels (e.g., 90% committed / 50% planned / 20% exploring) so sales learns which items they may put in contracts.
- Keep a visible "not doing" list with reasons; it kills the same request being re-raised quarterly and documents the strategy's edges.
- Version the roadmap and changelog it — "what changed since last quarter and why" builds more stakeholder trust than pretending nothing slipped.

## Pitfalls
- Date-and-feature roadmaps shared with customers, converting estimates into contractual expectations that then drive death-march delivery.
- The "roadmap as wish list": every stakeholder's item appears somewhere in "Later," so the roadmap makes no choices and prioritization happens invisibly at sprint level.
- Planning to 100% engineering capacity, guaranteeing that the first production incident or hiring slip cascades through every commitment.
