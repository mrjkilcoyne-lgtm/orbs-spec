# OKRs

## Scope
Setting and running Objectives and Key Results: goal architecture, scoring, cadence, and keeping OKRs from degenerating into task lists.

## Core principles
- The Objective is qualitative and inspiring; Key Results are quantitative proof the objective happened ("as measured by" — Doerr's formula: "I will [Objective] as measured by [KRs]"); if a KR can be "done" without any number moving, it's a task, not a KR.
- KRs must be outcomes, not output: "ship the referral program" is a milestone; "referral-sourced signups reach 15% of new users" is a KR — output OKRs recreate the feature factory with extra ceremony.
- Fewer is more: 1-3 objectives with 2-4 KRs each per team; ten objectives means no priorities, and every added OKR dilutes the forcing function.
- Committed vs. aspirational must be labeled (Google's distinction): committed OKRs expect 1.0 and trigger escalation at risk; aspirational OKRs target 0.7 average — grading both on one scale punishes ambition or excuses sandbagging.
- OKRs are not compensation inputs; the moment bonuses attach to OKR scores, teams sandbag targets (Goodhart's law) and the system measures negotiation skill, not impact.

## Apex practices
- Set OKRs 60/40 top-down/bottom-up: leadership names the objectives' direction, teams draft their own KRs — pure cascade kills ownership, pure bottom-up loses alignment.
- Run weekly check-ins scoring confidence (on-track/at-risk/off-track) per KR, not just quarter-end grading; the mid-quarter "we're at 0.2 with 6 weeks left" conversation is the entire point.
- Pair every growth KR with a health/guardrail metric (e.g., activation up AND support tickets flat) so teams can't hit the number by breaking something adjacent.
- Draft KRs with baselines in the sentence ("from 22% to 30%," not "improve activation"); a KR without a baseline is a wish with a deadline.

## Pitfalls
- The OKR-to-task-list slide: by week 3 the KRs are Jira epics and the quarter is graded on completion percentage of planned work.
- Setting Q1 OKRs in February because "we needed the roadmap first" — OKRs that follow the plan rubber-stamp it instead of shaping it.
- Cascading KRs verbatim down the org chart so a support team owns a revenue number it cannot influence; alignment means contribution, not arithmetic decomposition.

## Tools & references
Doerr "Measure What Matters," Google's OKR playbook (rework.withgoogle.com), Christina Wodtke "Radical Focus," Felipe Castro's OKR guides; Goodhart's law as the standing caveat.
