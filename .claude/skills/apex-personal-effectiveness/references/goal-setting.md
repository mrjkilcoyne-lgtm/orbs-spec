# Goal Setting

## Scope
Choosing, framing, and tracking goals so they change behavior: goal hierarchies, SMART/OKR-style framing, and systems vs. outcomes.

## Core principles
- Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory (40+ years of evidence): specific, difficult goals outperform "do your best" goals — but only with commitment, feedback, and adequate ability; past that, difficulty without feedback just demoralizes.
- Goals set direction; systems produce progress (Clear's formulation): "write a book" does nothing daily, "500 words before 9am" does — every outcome goal needs a process goal underneath it.
- Goodhart's Law applies to yourself: when a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure — pair every quantitative goal with a countervailing quality check (e.g., "ship 4 features" with "zero P1 regressions").
- Approach goals beat avoidance goals: "run 3x/week" sustains motivation where "stop being sedentary" does not; the brain optimizes toward targets, not away from voids.
- Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer): the "if situation X, then action Y" format roughly doubles follow-through versus intention alone — a goal without a trigger-action plan is a preference, not a plan.

## Apex practices
- Limit to 3 active goals per horizon (quarter, year); Warren Buffett's apocryphal 25/5 exercise holds — the "avoid-at-all-costs" list of good-but-not-top goals is what protects the top ones.
- Write goals as OKRs: qualitative objective plus 2–4 measurable key results scored 0.0–1.0, targeting ~0.7 (sandbagging to 1.0 means you aimed too low).
- Do a weekly scoring ritual: 10 minutes rating progress and choosing next week's lead actions — goals reviewed less than weekly decay into decoration.
- Pre-mortem each major goal (Klein): "it's six months later and this failed — why?" surfaces the obstacles implementation intentions should target.

## Pitfalls
- Setting cascading goals so aggressive that gaming the metric becomes the rational strategy (Wells Fargo accounts scandal is the canonical case).
- Confusing goals with values — a goal is achievable and done; treating "be healthy" as a goal instead of a value produces neither plan nor endpoint.
- Public commitment to identity-level goals ("I'm becoming a founder") can substitute for action — symbolic self-completion; share plans, not identities.

## Tools & references
Locke & Latham "A Theory of Goal Setting," Doerr's "Measure What Matters" (OKRs), Clear's "Atomic Habits," Gollwitzer's implementation-intentions research, quarterly OKR docs.
