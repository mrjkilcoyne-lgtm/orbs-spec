# Focus & Attention

## Scope
The mechanics of sustained attention: managing interruptions, task-switching costs, digital distraction, and attention training.

## Core principles
- Multitasking on cognitive tasks is rapid task-switching with a toll: each switch costs reorientation time and error rate (Rubinstein/Monsell), and heavy media multitaskers perform worse on switching itself (Ophir, Nass & Wagner, 2009) — the practice degrades the capacity.
- Interruptions cost more than their duration: after an interruption, returning to the original task takes an average of ~23 minutes (Mark, UC Irvine), and people compensate by working faster with more stress and errors.
- Attention is cue-driven before it's willed: notifications exploit variable-ratio reinforcement (the slot-machine schedule, the strongest known conditioning schedule), so removing cues beats resisting them — silence is an environment property, not a virtue.
- The prefrontal system fatigues within a session but the "ego depletion" strong claim failed replication — what's robust is that boredom, blood glucose crashes, and unresolved open loops (Zeigarnik effect) degrade focus; manage those inputs rather than rationing a mystical willpower tank.
- Mind-wandering occupies ~47% of waking thought (Killingsworth & Gilbert) and is the default state; focus is the trained exception — brief mindfulness training measurably improves sustained-attention task performance and reduces wandering.

## Apex practices
- Make the phone physically absent: mere presence of one's smartphone reduces available working memory even when it's face-down and silent (Ward et al., 2017, "brain drain") — another room, not another pocket.
- Batch communication into declared windows and set status accordingly; asynchronous-by-default norms cut interruption load an order of magnitude more than personal discipline does.
- Use work/rest intervals matched to the task: Pomodoro (25/5) for resistance-heavy work, 90-minute ultradian blocks for flow-capable work — the interval's job is a pre-committed start, which is the hardest part.
- Capture intrusions instead of following them: a "distraction pad" beside the keyboard — write the errant thought, return to task; this closes the loop without the switch.

## Pitfalls
- Keeping email/chat open in a second monitor "just to glance" — a standing invitation to attention residue on every glance.
- Confusing stimulation with focus: lo-fi playlists and busy cafés can help some tasks, but for verbal working-memory tasks, background speech and lyrics measurably impair performance (irrelevant-speech effect).
- Blaming character rather than architecture: an environment engineered for capture (autoplay, infinite scroll, badges) defeats intention; audit the environment first.

## Tools & references
