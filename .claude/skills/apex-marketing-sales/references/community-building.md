# Community Building

## Scope
Creating engaged groups of users and advocates: Discord/Slack communities, forums, user groups, and customer councils.

## Core principles
- Communities reduce churn by increasing stickiness (engaged users in a community are 5x less likely to churn); they also reduce support cost (users answer other users' questions).
- Community thrives on unequal participation (Pareto distribution): 10% of members create 90% of content; don't try to get everyone equally active — enable the 10% to lead.
- Community needs a shared identity and values; a Discord for "Stripe users" is vague, but "Stripe users building SaaS in emerging markets" creates cohesion.
- Moderation and governance matter; a community that tolerates spam, toxicity, or self-promotion becomes a ghost town. Set norms and enforce them.
- Community ROI is lagged and qualitative (customer insights, retention, word-of-mouth) before being quantitative (revenue contribution). Quantifying community too early kills its authentic purpose.

## Apex practices
- Start with a small Discord or Slack community (50-100 power users) and curate quality over growth; a thriving 200-person community beats a ghost 5,000-person Slack.
- Have a community manager (or empower a power user to co-lead) who sets norms, seeds discussions, and removes bad actors; organic communities without leadership fail.
- Create rituals (weekly ask-me-anything, monthly virtual meetup, annual conference) that give people reasons to return.
- Tie community participation to company decisions (early access to features, input on roadmap) so members feel their participation matters.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming a community will scale itself; communities need investment until they reach critical mass (500-1000 active members).
- Monetizing too aggressively (premium tiers, ads) and killing the vibe; communities should feel like a gift, not a hustle.
- Building a community around the company instead of the user's needs; "Product Users Community" fails, "Data Engineers Community" succeeds even if your product is the vehicle.
