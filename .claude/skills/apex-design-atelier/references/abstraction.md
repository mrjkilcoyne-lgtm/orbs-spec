# Abstraction

## Scope
Meaning without depiction — Kandinsky's inner necessity, Malevich's zero point, through Abstract Expressionism — and abstraction as the designer's daily act.

## Core principles
- Abstraction is compression toward essence: remove likeness until only the operative relationships (tension, balance, rhythm) remain — the same act as turning a workflow into an icon.
- Kandinsky's claim: color and form act directly on the psyche like music; a composition can be "played" without depicting anything. Test: does the layout still communicate with the labels removed?
- Malevich's zero point (Black Square): there is a floor — the minimum mark that is still a statement. Knowing where zero is calibrates every decision above it.
- Two temperatures: geometric abstraction (order, system, Mondrian/Malevich) vs gestural abstraction (energy, body, Pollock/de Kooning). Choose the temperature before the composition.
- Non-representational ≠ non-referential: Rothko denied being an abstractionist — the abstraction carries subject matter (grief, ecstasy) by other means. Abstraction without intended content is decoration.

## Apex practices
- Abstract by subtraction with a stop rule: remove one element at a time, stop the step before meaning collapses — that's the essence; one step further is the logo.
- Name the felt quality first ("anxious energy," "calm authority"), then choose form-language (jagged/soft, dense/sparse) to embody it — never the reverse.
- Use scale relationships, not objects, to build hierarchy: abstraction's toolkit is size, weight, interval, direction.
- Squint test and thumbnail test: real abstract structure survives being 40px tall; incidental detail doesn't.

## Pitfalls
- Vague abstraction defended as "open to interpretation" — openness is designed, not defaulted into.
- Gestural marks generated mechanically (fake spontaneity reads instantly as fake).
- Abstracting the wrong thing: keeping the look of the source and discarding its structure.

## Tools & references
Kandinsky "Concerning the Spiritual in Art," "Point and Line to Plane," Malevich's Suprematism manifesto, MoMA Ab-Ex collection, Bridget Riley's writings.
