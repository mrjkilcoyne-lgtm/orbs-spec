# Photorealism

## Scope
Close/Estes/Flack's photographic fidelity as discipline — precision, patience, and the decision about what deserves that devotion; the craft of the high-fidelity render.

## Core principles
- Photorealism is not copying; it's translation with total accountability — every square centimeter is a deliberate decision at maximum resolution. In design: the polished final comp, the production-grade render.
- The grid method (Chuck Close): monumental fidelity is achieved cell by cell — break the impossible whole into finishable units, each verifiable against the source. Precisely the engineer's decomposition discipline.
- The camera sees differently than the eye: depth-of-field blur, specular highlights, lens distortion. Photorealists paint the photograph, not the scene — fidelity means fidelity to a chosen intermediate representation.
- Surfaces are physics: convincing realism lives in material response — how chrome, glass, skin each return light. (The same truth games and PBR rendering formalized.)
- Subject banality is the point: Estes's diners and storefronts say the ordinary withstands infinite attention. Devotional fidelity confers importance — spend it deliberately.

## Apex practices
- Work from reference always; "from imagination" photorealism drifts into symbol (the remembered eye, not the seen one). Designers: real content, real data, real device frames — lorem ipsum hides the truth.
- Verify by difference: flip between work and reference rapidly (or overlay/difference-blend); errors invisible to staring pop instantly.
- Establish values before detail: a value-correct blurry underpainting beats sharp details on wrong values — resolve global light first, then zoom in.
- Budget fidelity: photoreal costs 10x; reserve it for the hero surface and let supporting elements stay gestural — contrast of finish directs the eye.

## Pitfalls
- Uncanny valley from mixed fidelity: one photoreal element beside flat graphics without an integrating shadow/grade.
- Detail as procrastination: rendering eyelashes while the drawing's proportions are wrong.
- Fidelity to a bad photograph — the source must be art-directed as hard as the render.

## Tools & references
Chuck Close's grid portraits, Richard Estes's reflections, Audrey Flack's vanitas still lifes; PBR references, "Color and Light" (Gurney) for the physics.
