# Learning How to Learn

## Scope
Meta-learning: the evidence-based techniques that make skill and knowledge acquisition faster and more durable.

## Core principles
- Retrieval practice beats re-reading by a wide margin (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006): testing yourself is the study method, not the assessment of it — recognition feels like knowledge but recall is knowledge.
- Desirable difficulties (Bjork): conditions that slow learning during practice — spacing, interleaving, variation — improve retention and transfer; fluency during study is a misleading cue.
- Interleaving beats blocking: mixing problem types (ABCABC not AAABBB) forces discrimination between methods, which is the actual skill exams and life demand (Rohrer's math studies).
- Focused and diffuse modes alternate (Oakley): stuck problems yield to deliberate disengagement — walks, sleep — because the diffuse network keeps working; grinding past the stall point is negative-yield.
- Transfer is narrow by default: learning chess doesn't improve general reasoning (Thorndike onward); to get transfer, practice the varied contexts you actually want the skill to work in.

## Apex practices
- After any study session, close the book and free-recall everything onto blank paper, then check gaps — the Feynman technique variant that exposes illusion of competence in minutes.
- Space deliberately: revisit material at expanding intervals (1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month) rather than massing; a scheduler (Anki) automates the arithmetic.
- Learn by making errors first: attempt problems before studying solutions (productive failure, Kapur) — pretesting improves subsequent encoding even when the attempt fails.
- For skills, use deliberate practice structure (Ericsson): isolate a subskill just beyond current ability, get immediate feedback, repeat with refinement — hours of unstructured repetition plateau.

## Pitfalls
- Highlighting and re-reading as primary methods — the two most popular and least effective techniques in Dunlosky et al.'s (2013) review.
- Matching teaching to "learning styles" (visual/auditory/kinesthetic) — repeatedly debunked; match modality to the content, not the learner.
- Consuming courses and books without production: understanding is built by generating (writing, coding, explaining), not by exposure.

## Tools & references
