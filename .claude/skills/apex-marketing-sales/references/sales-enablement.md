# Sales Enablement

## Scope
Tools, content, and coaching that help sales reps close deals faster: playbooks, collateral, training, and performance coaching.

## Core principles
- Sales enablement is not HR training; it's the operational infrastructure that removes friction from selling (product knowledge, messaging, customer lists, proposal templates, competitive positioning).
- A top rep's playbook (how they prospect, what they say, how they handle objections) is replicable; codify it in documentation and training so the rest of the team reaches 70% of top rep performance.
- Content (case studies, ROI calculators, competitive comparisons, product sheets) must be kept current and used by reps; dusty collateral is worse than no collateral (signals neglect).
- Coaching (observing calls, providing feedback) is where reps improve fastest; a rep who gets 1 coaching call per week outperforms one who gets annual training.
- Sales tools (CRM, dialer, proposal software) should reduce, not increase, admin burden; if reps spend 2 hours per day in CRM and 3 hours on the phone, the tools aren't enabling.

## Apex practices
- Create a sales playbook per buyer persona (who they are, what they care about, how to position, common objections, close tactics) and update quarterly as you learn.
- Build a content library (case studies, ROI calculator, competitive positioning) and integrate it into the sales process (send case study after discovery call, calculator during proposal stage).
- Implement weekly team coaching (recorded calls, feedback, best practice sharing); this is the fastest way to improve team average.
- Measure enablement ROI via ramp time (how long until a new rep reaches productivity) and team average deal size; if onboarding used to take 6 months and enablement brings it to 3 months, that's millions in value.

## Pitfalls
- Building playbooks in a vacuum without reps' input; the best playbooks come from observing top reps and codifying what they do.
- Treating sales enablement as annual training; continuous coaching compounds more than annual events.
- Creating content without distribution; a case study nobody uses is a waste of marketing effort.
