# Business Taxes

## Scope
Corporate, payroll, and sales tax obligations: entity structure selection, tax planning, compliance, and common deductions.

## Core principles
- Entity structure (C-corp, S-corp, LLC) determines tax treatment; C-corps pay corporate tax then shareholders pay tax on dividends (double taxation), but S-corps and LLCs avoid this via pass-through taxation.
- Depreciation and amortization create tax deductions for capital investments; $100k capex in year 1 might be deducted over 5 years ($20k/year), reducing taxable income without reducing cash.
- Tax loss carryforwards (NOLs — net operating losses) accumulate when the company operates at a loss and can be used to offset future profits; early-stage startups accumulate NOLs that reduce tax burden in profitable years.
- Sales tax nexus (obligation to collect sales tax) depends on physical presence and transaction volume; remote sellers now have nexus in all states (post-South Dakota v. Wayfair), creating compliance burden.
- Estimated quarterly tax payments are required when income doesn't have withholding; miss payments and the IRS assesses penalties on top of taxes.

## Apex practices
- Hire a CPA or tax advisor during incorporation; an hour of consultation saves 10x more than the cost in missed deductions or wrong entity structure.
- Implement payroll software (Guidepoint, ADP, Paychex) to handle withholding, filings, and compliance; manual payroll is a compliance minefield.
- Document R&D spending (engineers working on internal projects, depreciation on lab equipment) to claim the R&D tax credit (20% credit on qualifying spending).
- Plan for equity taxation early; exercising ISOs (incentive stock options) requires understanding AMT (alternative minimum tax) and the implications of early exercise.

## Pitfalls
- Failing to file quarterly estimated taxes and then owing huge penalties; the IRS charges penalties on the tax owed, not just the tax itself.
- Mixing personal and business finances; the IRS disallows deductions for personal expenses and commingling invites full audit.
- Assuming you'll handle taxes later; year-end scrambles for receipts and documentation lead to missed deductions and errors.

## Tools & references
