# Plain Language

## Scope
Writing clearly for broad audiences: short words, active voice, and removing jargon without talking down.

## Core principles
- Plain language is not simple language; it's respectful language that prioritizes the reader's time and comprehension over sounding smart.
- Average sentence length < 15 words and average word length < 5 letters correlate with comprehension. This doesn't mean all sentences are short, but excess length hides meaning.
- Jargon (specialized vocabulary) excludes; when jargon is necessary, define it once per document or link to glossary.
- Active voice ("We improved response time") beats passive voice ("Response time was improved") because it names the actor and is shorter.
- Pronouns (you, we, I) create intimacy; use "you" when the reader is the subject (benefits, actions, decisions); it pulls readers in.

## Apex practices
- Test readability with tools (Hemingway Editor, Flesch-Kincaid) but don't blindly optimize; sound natural while keeping sentences short.
- Use headings and lists to break up walls of text; scanning (not reading) is the default.
- Replace nominalization (the implementation of the process) with verbs (we implemented the process).
- Read aloud; your ear catches awkwardness that your eyes miss.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming more words = more credibility; verbosity buries meaning and tires readers.
- Oversimplifying to the point of inaccuracy; "plain" doesn't mean "false."
- Using too many acronyms; spell out first reference (U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, EPA), then abbreviate.

## Tools & references
Plain Language Association, Strunk-White "Elements of Style," Nielsen's "Writing for the Web," Flesch Readability Ease score.
