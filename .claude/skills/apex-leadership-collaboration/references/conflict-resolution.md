# Conflict Resolution

## Scope
Surfacing, navigating, and resolving interpersonal and inter-team conflict at work — including the conflicts worth having.

## Core principles
- Task conflict (about the work) is healthy and correlates with better decisions; relationship conflict (about the people) is corrosive — the leader's job is keeping the first from curdling into the second, and absence of all conflict signals fear, not health (Lencioni's "artificial harmony").
- Separate positions from interests (Fisher & Ury): "we must use Kafka" vs "I can't have another 3am pager storm" — positions collide, interests often compose; almost every stuck argument is two positions hiding compatible interests.
- The fundamental attribution error runs both directions: you explain their behavior by character ("he's territorial") and yours by circumstance ("I was under deadline") — assume a situational explanation exists and go find it before assigning motive.
- Conflicts escalate through predictable stages (Glasl's model: from debate, to actions-not-words, to reputation attacks, to open war); intervention that works at stage 2 (facilitated conversation) fails at stage 5 (needs mediation with authority) — diagnose the stage before choosing the tool.
- Unaddressed conflict doesn't stay still, it goes underground: the visible argument becomes invisible non-cooperation, information hoarding, and slow-rolled reviews — the cost of avoidance compounds silently.

## Apex practices
- Get the parties to state the other side's view to the other's satisfaction before advocating their own (steelmanning as a gate) — half of conflicts partially dissolve at this step because they were misunderstandings wearing armor.
- Use Crucial Conversations mechanics: start with facts not conclusions, tentatively share your story ("the story I'm telling myself is..."), and restore safety the moment you see silence or violence responses.
- For inter-team conflict, look for the structural cause first: misaligned incentives, unclear ownership, or a shared dependency — most "personality clashes" between teams are org design bugs with faces.
- As a manager, resist adjudicating: bring the parties together and facilitate; every conflict you settle by fiat teaches the team to escalate instead of resolve, and you become the bottleneck for peace.

## Pitfalls
- Triangulation: A complains to you about B, you talk to B for them — you've become the transmission medium and both relationships now route through you; coach A to go direct (with support if needed).
- Papering over with a compromise nobody believes in ("split the difference") — both parties leave with 50% resentment, and the issue returns with interest.
- Public confrontation of relationship conflict: task disagreements can be argued in the open; anything touching competence or character goes private, always.

## Tools & references
Fisher & Ury "Getting to Yes," Patterson et al. "Crucial Conversations," Stone/Patton/Heen "Difficult Conversations," Glasl's conflict escalation model, Lencioni "The Five Dysfunctions of a Team."
