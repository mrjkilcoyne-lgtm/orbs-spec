# Business Models

## Scope
Revenue and profit architectures that determine company economics: subscription, transactional, marketplace, freemium, licensing, and hybrids.

## Core principles
- Business models determine unit economics and capital requirements; subscription requires upfront customer acquisition investment but creates predictable, recurring revenue; transactional requires repeat engagement but is fragile to one-time disruption.
- Gross margin is the first filter: 80%+ gross margin (SaaS) allows for high customer acquisition costs and early losses; <30% gross margin (logistics) requires either high volume or pricing power to survive.
- Customer concentration risk varies by model: a marketplace (Airbnb) is vulnerable if major suppliers leave; a transactional model (Stripe) is resilient because each transaction is independent.
- Monetization timing matters: YouTube took years to monetize; Stripe monetized from inception (2% take rate). Early monetization clarifies unit economics but risks product-market fit if paywall is too aggressive.
- Network effects strengthen certain models (two-sided marketplaces, social networks) but are weak in others (transactional B2B); choosing a model that benefits from network effects creates defensibility.

## Apex practices
- Design the model with unit economics in mind: if SaaS, target CAC payback < 12 months; if marketplace, target take rate that funds ops without destroying supply.
- Test model switches early (subscription vs. transactional, freemium vs. free-trial); changing later dilutes focus and confuses customers.
- Bundle features or services to enable price discrimination (freemium → premium, standard → enterprise) and capture value from high-willingness-to-pay segments.
- Understand switching costs in your model; subscription creates switching costs (data, workflow), transactional models don't — price elasticity differs.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming a successful startup requires subscription revenue; plenty of profitable companies run on transactions (Shopify merchants) or one-time sales (professional services).
- Monetizing too aggressively before product-market fit (freemium paywall kills engagement) or too late (free products become habits, paying for habits is hard).
- Mixing models without clear separation; Slack is subscription + marketplace (app store); mixing creates accounting and UX confusion if not intentional.

## Tools & references
