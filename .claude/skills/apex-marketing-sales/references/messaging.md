# Messaging

## Scope
The words, frames, and stories used to communicate value to buyers: messaging architecture, proof points, and narrative consistency.

## Core principles
- Messaging must match the buyer's worldview, not your product's architecture; if the buyer thinks "integration is hard," explain how you solve that, not that you have "modular APIs."
- Proof points (data, customer quotes, case studies) anchor abstract claims; "3x faster" means nothing; "processes that took 2 hours now take 40 minutes" and "customers process 30% more transactions in the same time" are credible.
- Messaging architecture (primary message, 3-5 supporting messages, proof points per message) provides consistency across sales, marketing, and product teams; every channel shouldn't invent new messaging.
- Emotional and rational messaging both matter; rational (ROI, speed) gets the buyer's attention, emotional (confidence, control, belonging) drives the decision.
- Messaging fatigue is real; rotating core messages every 3-6 months keeps them fresh; keeping the same message for 18 months often reduces effectiveness.

## Apex practices
- Build a messaging house (core positioning, key message, supporting messages, proof points, objection handler per message) and socialize it across teams; this is your forcing function for alignment.
- Test messaging with real buyers before launching campaigns; a/b test subject lines, headlines, and proofs in email or ads before big spend.
- Use specific numbers and timeframes (15% improvement in 90 days) instead of vague benefits (significantly improved efficiency).
- Create messaging variants by buyer role (CFO cares about cost, CTO cares about speed); one message doesn't resonate across all buyers.

## Pitfalls
- Changing messaging based on one piece of feedback; validate trends with 5-10 interviews before iterating.
- Using jargon ("synergistic paradigm") instead of simple language; clarity beats cleverness.
- Over-promising in messaging and under-delivering in product; trust is harder to rebuild than to build first.

## Tools & references
