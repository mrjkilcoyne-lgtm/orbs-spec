# Biology Fundamentals

## Scope
Cell structure, genetics, evolution, metabolism, and homeostasis; the central dogma (DNA → RNA → protein) and energy flow in ecosystems.

## Core principles
- Cells are the unit of life; prokaryotes (bacteria) lack a nucleus, eukaryotes (animals, plants, fungi) have one. Cell membranes are selectively permeable lipid bilayers.
- The central dogma: DNA (genes) encodes information → RNA (messenger) carries instructions → proteins (catalysts, structure) do work. Mutations in DNA alter downstream proteins.
- Evolution by natural selection: heritable variation + differential reproduction = change in allele frequency over generations. Fitness is reproductive success in an environment.
- Metabolism: anabolic pathways build (glycogenesis, protein synthesis); catabolic pathways break down (glycolysis, oxidation) to harvest energy (ATP). Energy flows from sun → producers → consumers.
- Homeostasis: organisms maintain stable internal conditions (pH, temperature, osmolarity) via feedback loops; allostasis adjusts set-points for new environments.

## Apex practices
- Think in terms of flux: energy flow, nutrient cycling, gene flow. Steady state is dynamic equilibrium (flows in = flows out), not stasis.
- Use Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (allele frequencies stable without mutation/selection/drift/migration) as a null model; deviations reveal evolutionary forces.
- Understand feedback (negative stabilizes, positive amplifies) in hormonal and genetic regulation; positive feedback loops are rare and temporary (coagulation, cell division).
- Recognize that correlation in biology (trait A associated with trait B) often reflects selection, pleiotropy (one gene affects both), or linkage; not causation.

## Pitfalls
- Confusing genotype (genetic makeup) with phenotype (observable traits); environment affects phenotype (and even gene expression via epigenetics).
- Assuming adaptation is perfect; organisms are shaped by historical constraints, trade-offs, and drift; not every feature is an adaptation.
- Ignoring that evolution has no goal or foresight; "design" terminology in biology can mislead—it's the result of mindless selection.

## Tools & references
Alberts "Molecular Biology of the Cell," Darwin's "Origin of Species," population genetics (Hardy-Weinberg, allele frequency), bioinformatics basics.
