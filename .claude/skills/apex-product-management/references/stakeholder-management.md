# Stakeholder Management

## Scope
Building the alignment and trust that lets product decisions stick: mapping influence, managing up and across, and handling executive intervention.

## Core principles
- Map stakeholders on power × interest and treat the quadrants differently: high-power/high-interest get co-creation, high-power/low-interest get concise proactive updates — one-size communication under-serves the people who can kill your roadmap.
- Alignment is manufactured before the meeting, not in it: the pre-read plus 1:1 pre-wires (Amazon-style narrative memos, "no surprises" rule) mean the big meeting ratifies; a decision first argued live in front of 12 people becomes a status contest.
- Stakeholders oppose plans for reasons that are rational in their incentive structure — sales wants the deal-blocker fixed because they're comped on it; find the underlying interest (Fisher & Ury: positions vs. interests) and trade there.
- Trust compounds through predicted behavior: saying what you'll do, doing it, and flagging slips early is the entire mechanism; a PM who surprises stakeholders twice loses roadmap authority regardless of judgment quality.
- Escalation is a tool, not a failure: a crisp, jointly-framed escalation ("we disagree on X, here are both options and the decision needed by Friday") is healthier than months of passive-aggressive slow-rolling.

## Apex practices
- Run a monthly stakeholder tour (rotating 1:1s with sales, support, marketing, eng leads) asking "what's coming that I can't see yet?" — upstream intelligence beats damage control.
- Decide with an explicit decision protocol (DACI/RAPID): name the single Driver/Decider per decision before debate starts; ambiguity about WHO decides generates more conflict than the decision itself.
- Convert the loudest critics into inputs: give the skeptical sales VP a named slot in roadmap reviews and attribute wins to their input — co-ownership is cheaper than opposition.
- Write decision docs with options considered and rejected (and why); the paper trail prevents relitigating and shows stakeholders their option was weighed, not ignored.

## Pitfalls
- The order-taker slide: converting every executive request directly into roadmap items until the roadmap is a political ledger and the PM is a ticket router.
- Managing only upward while alienating support, design, and engineering peers — the people who control whether commitments actually ship.
- Winning the meeting but losing the sponsor: steamrolling a stakeholder in public buys one decision at the cost of an opponent who now works the back channel.

## Tools & references
DACI (Atlassian) / RAPID (Bain), Fisher & Ury "Getting to Yes," power-interest grids (Mendelow), Amazon 6-pager/pre-read culture, Bruce Tuckman + "Crucial Conversations" for the hard talks.
