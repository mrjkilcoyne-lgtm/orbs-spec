# Professional Networking

## Scope
Building relationships across your industry or adjacent fields: maintaining weak ties, creating opportunities through connection, and becoming a node in networks that matter to your career.

## Core principles
- Weak ties (acquaintances, not close friends) are the primary source of job opportunities and novel information (Granovetter's research); your close circle knows what you know, but your periphery knows what you don't.
- Giving first, without expectation of immediate return, builds credibility and reciprocity (Cialdini); people help those who've helped them, and that compounds over years.
- A consistent, low-friction presence (monthly coffee, annual conference, shared Slack community) beats sporadic intensity — relationships are built through repeated, positive contact.
- Introductions multiply your network exponentially because other people's networks become your networks; asking for and providing warm introductions is the most valuable currency.
- Visibility outside your organization (GitHub contributions, conference talks, blog, public work) creates inbound opportunity so you're not fishing alone — it serves as constant passive networking.

## Apex practices
- Schedule a recurring monthly coffee with 1–2 people from your network (peers, senior folks you admire, people in adjacent domains); 12 coffees/year keeps relationships warm and creates touchstones.
- Contribute meaningfully to 1–2 communities or groups (open-source, industry association, Slack community, mastermind group) so you're known for substance, not just showing up.
- When meeting someone, ask what they're working on and remember one detail to follow up on later (email after a week: "How did the X project go?"); most people don't follow up at all.
- Facilitate introductions freely: when you meet someone doing X and recall someone else working on X-related problems, make the warm intro (you become the connector, which is high-value).

## Pitfalls
- Networking as transactional ("I need a job, let me call people") — it reads as desperate and burns bridges; relationships can't be rushed.
- Collecting business cards or LinkedIn connections without substance — 500 shallow connections don't compare to 10 real relationships.
- Assuming people owe you help because you've been in the same space; you must have done something for them or be willing to help them first.

## Tools & references
Granovetter's "The Strength of Weak Ties," Cialdini's "Influence," LinkedIn for basic maintenance, Slack/Discord communities for your field, conference programs (identify people before attending), CalendlySync for scheduling.
