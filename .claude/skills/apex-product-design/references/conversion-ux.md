# Conversion UX

## Scope
Optimizing conversion funnels: friction points, persuasive design, behavioral psychology, and measuring impact.

## Core principles
- Conversion depends on reducing friction (steps, fields, complexity) and increasing clarity (why should you care, what's the benefit?).
- Social proof (reviews, testimonials, usage numbers) and scarcity (limited time, limited supply) influence decisions; use honestly, not manipulatively.
- Defaults and suggestions shape choices; 80% of users don't change defaults — set good defaults and suggest recommended options.
- Form friction is massive; every additional field reduces completion by ~5% (empirical data) — only ask for essential data.
- Messaging (copy, headlines, buttons) changes conversion by 10–30%; "Start Free Trial" outperforms "Submit Form" because it promises benefit.

## Apex practices
- Run A/B tests on high-impact pages; test one variable at a time (headline, button color, form fields) to isolate what changed.
- Measure at each step: landing → signup → first action; identify where the funnel breaks and iterate that step.
- Test variations (heatmaps, session recordings) to find friction; Hotjar or Clarity show where users click, pause, or abandon.
- Use behavioral triggers (timing, scroll depth, intent) to show offers; a "help" popup at the right moment increases completion.

## Pitfalls
- Over-optimizing for conversion at the expense of user trust; dark patterns (misleading copy, trick buttons) convert short-term but harm long-term retention.
- Trusting intuition over data; test counterintuitive changes; orange buttons don't always outperform blue.
- Not considering mobile; a desktop conversion-optimized form is usually too long for mobile.

## Tools & references
Google Optimize, Optimizely, Unbounce for A/B testing, Hotjar for heatmaps, Crazy Egg, "Contagious" (Jonah Berger); behavioral psychology in "Thinking Fast and Slow" (Kahneman).
