# Chemistry Fundamentals

## Scope
Atomic structure, chemical bonding, stoichiometry, equilibrium, kinetics, and redox reactions.

## Core principles
- Atoms have nuclei (protons, neutrons) and electrons; electrons in orbitals determine bonding and reactivity. Periodic table organizes by electron configuration.
- Chemical bonds (ionic, covalent, metallic, hydrogen) transfer or share electrons; bond strength and type determine molecular properties (polarity, solubility, melting point).
- Stoichiometry converts between moles and grams via atomic/molecular mass; reactions must balance atoms (conservation of mass and charge).
- Chemical equilibrium (K) quantifies how far forward a reaction goes; large K means mostly products, small K means mostly reactants; K is temperature-dependent (not concentration).
- Kinetics (rate laws, activation energy, catalysts) describes how fast reactions happen; equilibrium is silent on kinetics (fast vs. slow pathways to the same equilibrium).

## Apex practices
- Write balanced equations by counting atoms and charges; use stoichiometry to relate moles, masses, and concentrations.
- Use equilibrium expressions (K = [products]/[reactants]) to predict direction of change (Q vs. K); calculate how composition shifts to reach K.
- Understand Le Chatelier's principle: if you stress a system at equilibrium (add/remove concentration, pressure, heat), it shifts to counteract the stress.
- Recognize that kinetics and thermodynamics are independent: a reaction can be favorable (large K) but slow (high activation energy); catalysts speed equilibration without changing K.

## Pitfalls
- Confusing reactions that go "to completion" (very large K) with infinitely fast reactions; thermodynamic feasibility ≠ kinetic feasibility.
- Forgetting that K is dimensionless (depends on standard states); comparing K values requires consistent units.
- Assuming room temperature and standard pressure; temperature dramatically affects K; pressure affects gases and condensed phases differently.

## Tools & references
Atkins "Physical Chemistry," IUPAC nomenclature, Lewis structures and VSEPR theory for geometry, redox balancing by half-reactions.
