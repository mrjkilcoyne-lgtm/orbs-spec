# Illustration Fundamentals

## Scope
Drawing and digital painting: anatomy, perspective, value, gesture, and translating idea into image at various styles and scales.

## Core principles
- Gesture drawing (loose, quick, 30–60 seconds per sketch) trains your eye and hand to see proportion and movement before detail; it's the substrate all illustration builds from.
- Value (light-to-dark range) drives a successful illustration more than line quality; a simple shape with correct values reads; perfect lines with flat color don't.
- Perspective is a system for convincing the eye of 3D space on 2D surface; linear perspective (1-, 2-, 3-point) and atmospheric perspective (detail and color fade with distance) are learnable rules.
- Anatomy is structure: understanding underlying skeleton and muscle informs stylization; cartoonists and caricaturists draw "wrong" convincingly because they know the rules first.
- The finished illustration is interpretation, not photographic truth; style, medium, and intent should be unified so the image feels intentional, not "amateur trying to paint realistically."

## Apex practices
- Draw from life 3+ times weekly, focusing on different subjects (hands, faces, figure, architecture): observation is faster training than tutorials.
- Use reference images liberally but don't trace — study the reference, set it aside, draw from memory, check against the reference, repeat. This is how pros work.
- Establish values early (value study or underpainting) before adding color or detail; many illustrations struggle because value hierarchy is broken underneath.
- Develop a personal style deliberately by studying artists you admire, stealing techniques (not designs), and combining them with your interests and constraints.

## Pitfalls
- Skipping fundamentals (anatomy, perspective, value) to jump to finished paintings — the ceiling of your technical skill is your foundation level.
- Using tutorials as a substitute for observation — tutorials teach method, but drawing teaches seeing.
- Rendering every detail equally; illustration is composition and hierarchy, not realism.

## Tools & references
Hogarth's "Drawing the Head and Hands," Dodson's "Keys to Drawing," Bridgman's "Complete Guide to Life Drawing," Loomis' "Figure Drawing for All It's Worth," Procreate/Clip Studio Paint (digital), traditional media (graphite, charcoal, ink).
