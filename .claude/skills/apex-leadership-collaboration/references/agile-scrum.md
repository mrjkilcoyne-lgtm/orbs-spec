# Agile & Scrum

## Scope
Iterative delivery with Scrum's roles, events, and artifacts — and the agile principles underneath the ceremony.

## Core principles
- The Agile Manifesto's first line is the whole game: individuals and interactions over processes and tools — any Scrum implementation that adds ceremony while reducing conversation has inverted the priority it claims to serve.
- The sprint is a forecast, not a contract: its purpose is a short feedback loop against a Sprint Goal, and a sprint with ten unrelated tickets and no goal is just a two-week to-do list with extra meetings.
- Working software is the primary measure of progress — "done" must mean releasable (integrated, tested, deployable), because 90%-done inventory is where risk hides and velocity lies.
- Scrum deliberately separates the what (Product Owner orders the backlog) from the how (Developers own the sprint plan and estimates); when managers assign tasks into a sprint, you have status-report theater, not Scrum.
- Retrospectives are the improvement engine — a team that ships mediocre process but genuinely retros and changes one thing per sprint outruns a team with perfect ceremony and frozen habits (kaizen compounds).

## Apex practices
- Write Sprint Goals as outcomes ("users can reset passwords without support tickets"), then let scope flex within the sprint to protect the goal.
- Keep the backlog small and ruthlessly ordered: the top 10 items refined and estimable, everything below the fold allowed to be vague — refining item #87 is waste.
- Timebox refinement using historical data: three past sprints of throughput predict the next one better than any planning-poker marathon.
- Rotate retro formats (4Ls, sailboat, start-stop-continue) and track experiment follow-through; a retro whose actions never get done trains the team that reflection is decorative.

## Pitfalls
- Dark Scrum: standups as surveillance, velocity as a performance metric across teams, sprints as pressure devices — the mechanics of Scrum weaponized against its values.
- Cargo-culting SAFe or "scaled agile" bureaucracy onto three teams that needed a shared Slack channel and one roadmap.
- Comparing velocity between teams or "improving" it by inflating estimates; velocity is a local, relative planning signal, and Goodhart destroys it the moment it becomes a KPI.

## Tools & references
