# Retention

## Scope
Keeping users and revenue over time: retention measurement, activation, habit formation, resurrection, and churn diagnosis.

## Core principles
- Retention is the product-market-fit signal and the compounding base of all growth math: a flattening retention curve (asymptote > 0) means a durable core; a curve sliding to zero means acquisition is filling a leaky bucket and every growth investment is amortized churn.
- Measure retention on the product's natural frequency: a tax product judged on weekly retention looks dead and isn't; pick the usage interval (daily for messaging, weekly for PM tools, annual for tax) before reading any curve.
- Retention is made in the first session/week: activation (reaching the value moment fast) predicts long-term retention better than any later intervention — the cheapest churn fix is almost always upstream in onboarding, not in win-back emails.
- Distinguish the three churn species: voluntary product churn (value gap), involuntary churn (failed payments — often 20-40% of B2C SaaS churn, fixable with dunning), and contextual churn (job ended, company died) — each has a completely different remedy.
- Cohort curves, never blended rates: a "5% monthly churn" blends honeymoon cohorts with veterans; only stacked cohort curves reveal whether the product is actually getting stickier release over release.

## Apex practices
- Find the habit moment quantitatively: identify behaviors in week 1 that best separate month-6 retainers from churners, validate causally with experiments, then aim onboarding at that behavior.
- Build the resurrection channel deliberately: dormant-user win-backs keyed to new value shipped ("the thing you asked for now exists") outperform generic "we miss you" sends several-fold.
- Run churn autopsies as a standing ritual: exit surveys plus a monthly sample of 5-10 churned-customer interviews, coded into a reason taxonomy that feeds the roadmap — not a spreadsheet nobody reads.
- Fix involuntary churn first (smart dunning, card updater, retry logic, grace periods): it's the highest-ROI retention work in most subscription businesses because it needs no product change.

## Pitfalls
- Reporting logo retention when revenue retention tells the story (or vice versa): 95% logo retention with the whales churning is a crisis wearing a good number.
- Engagement hacks (streaks, notification pressure) that inflate short-term DAU while training users to resent the product — retention borrowed from the future.
- Treating churn as a save-team problem at cancellation time, when the decision was actually made 60 days earlier at the moment usage decayed — instrument the leading indicator, not the funeral.
