# Change Management

## Scope
Leading organizational shifts: migration from one system/process to another, managing resistance, pacing, and embedding the new normal.

## Core principles
- People don't resist change; they resist loss. Map what each group loses (power, skill relevance, familiar rhythms) and acknowledge it first.
- The emotional arc of change is predictable: denial → anger/resistance → bargaining → acceptance → integration; rushing past stages creates hidden pockets of sabotage.
- Early adopters and late adopters are different personas: what convinces one repels the other; message accordingly and avoid using the same people as referents.
- Change sticks only when aligned with incentives: if you reorg but compensation and goals stay the same, the new structure erodes within months.
- Parallel running (old and new system coexisting) feels expensive but prevents the "midnight cutover disaster"; budget for it.

## Apex practices
- Build a steering group of respected influencers from each affected group, not just executives; they surface friction you won't hear in town halls.
- Create a 90-day integration plan, not a cutover plan: announce → launch → support → institutionalize in distinct phases with clear go/no-go gates.
- Celebrate early wins visibly and tie them back to the change; "People started shipping 20% faster post-migration" is more motivating than "We unified our stack."
- Run smoke tests with a subset before full rollout; let early skeptics become witnesses to success.

## Pitfalls
- Over-communicating the "why" without enabling the "how"; people hear the rationale but lack tools or training to operate the new state.
- Announcing change without protecting early adopters from sabotage; if they get punished for pioneering, others won't follow.
- Treating the launch as completion; 80% of change work happens post-launch during the stabilization and embedding phase.

## Tools & references
Kotter's change leadership 8-step model, Bridges' transition curve, "Lean Change Management" by McHugh, stakeholder mapping for resistance points.
