# Copywriting

## Scope
Persuasive writing for marketing, product pages, emails, and ads: clarity, emotional resonance, and conversion.

## Core principles
- Copywriting is not about you; it's about the reader's desire, fear, or problem. Lead with what they want, not what you're selling.
- Specificity beats vague benefits: "Reduce response time by 40% for support tickets" outpulls "Faster support." Numbers, concrete examples, and vivid language stick.
- Scarcity and urgency are powerful but lose force with overuse; apply judiciously to genuine constraints (limited seats, deadline) not manufactured pressure.
- The headline is do-or-die; 5× as many people read headlines as body copy. Make the value proposition clear in 10 words or fewer.
- Social proof (testimonials, case studies, numbers like "10k companies use this") is persuasive because people trust peers more than vendors.

## Apex practices
- Use direct response testing (A/B tests on headlines, calls-to-action, copy length) to validate claims; gut feelings fail at scale.
- Write for scanners first: short paragraphs, bullet points, bold key phrases. Most readers skim; some read deeply.
- Use active voice and second-person ("you save time") rather than passive or passive-aggressive ("time is saved").
- Open with a hook: a surprising statistic, a question, or a bold claim. Then deliver on it immediately.

## Pitfalls
- Being clever or witty at the expense of clarity; puns don't convert, but clear value propositions do.
- Using jargon or corporate-speak ("synergize," "leverage") when plain language works better.
- Overpromising; disappointed readers become detractors. Underpromise slightly and overdeliver.

## Tools & references
Schwartz's "The Responsive Chord," Ogilvy's "Confessions of an Advertising Man," AIDA model (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action), A/B testing frameworks.
