# Podcasting

## Scope
Creating engaging audio content: conversation structure, guest preparation, pacing, production quality, and audience building.

## Core principles
- Podcasts are intimate; listeners hear your voice in their ears. Authenticity beats polish; over-production kills connection.
- Structured conversations (not meandering) hold attention: outline your throughline (main idea or narrative), signal topic shifts ("So let's talk about..."), and land your points before tangents.
- Silence and pauses are features, not bugs; they give listeners time to absorb, signal thinking, and feel natural. Rushing to fill silence sounds anxious.
- Audio-first thinking: without visuals, every concept must land aurally. Metaphors, anecdotes, and clear signposting matter more than in text.
- Consistency (release schedule, format, quality) builds habit-listening. Weekly episodes at the same time outpull sporadic surprises.

## Apex practices
- Interview prep: send guests a brief outline but not a script; it keeps energy live while ensuring you hit key points.
- Edit for flow and pacing: remove tangents that derailed the narrative, tighten pauses, add intro/outro consistency.
- Use show notes (written summary of key points and links); they help discoverability (Google indexes text) and let listeners follow up.
- Monitor analytics (listen time, drop-off points); long intros lose 30% of listeners; find your audience's patience threshold.

## Pitfalls
- Over-scripting interviews; chemistry comes from spontaneity, not reading statements back and forth.
- Hosting alone without structure; solo shows need stronger narrative arcs and more frequent signposting than interviews.
- Treating the outro as an afterthought; it's when listeners decide whether to subscribe. Make it compelling.

## Tools & references
Riverside.fm or SquadCast (quality recording), Descript (transcription + editing), Transistor or Anchor (hosting + distribution).
