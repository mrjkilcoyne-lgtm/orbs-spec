# Edtech

## Scope
Educational platforms: student progress tracking, course content delivery, assessment and grading, and analytics for learning outcomes.

## Core principles
- Learning data is sensitive (FERPA in US, GDPR in EU, state-level laws): student records require privacy, parental consent (under 13), and access controls; data breach costs reputational and legal.
- Progress is non-linear: students learn at different paces, mastery is not binary (passing a test ≠ understanding), and intervention timing matters; naive grading (average of scores) misses struggling students.
- Engagement metrics are misleading: time-on-task, completions, and quiz scores don't predict learning; evidence-based metrics (mistake patterns, prerequisite understanding) are harder to measure but more predictive.
- Content fragmentation (video, quiz, reading, interactive) is real; sequencing and prerequisites must be tracked (a student can't do algebra without arithmetic mastery).
- Assessment is cultural: standardized tests are one measure, but portfolios, peer feedback, and self-assessment reveal different learning; platforms must support multiple assessment types.

## Apex practices
- Track mastery, not just completion: record not just "passed" but "passed on attempt 3" or "passed with 72%"; interventions depend on detail.
- Implement adaptive learning (adjusting difficulty based on performance); a student who aces every problem is bored; one who fails every problem is frustrated.
- Use spaced repetition (revisit concepts at intervals) for retention; a course that teaches then never revisits doesn't stick.
- Integrate with student information systems (SIS) for roster, grades, and enrollment; manual sync is error-prone and duplicates work.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming engagement (watch time, clicks) correlates with learning; it often doesn't — assessment and outcomes matter.
- Forgetting that teachers are users too; tools must support grading, feedback, and class management, not just student learning.
- Ignoring accessibility (captions, alt text, keyboard navigation); it's required by law (ADA, Section 508) and expands audience.

## Tools & references
Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle (LMS platforms), Coursera, edX (MOOCs), FERPA (student records privacy), bloom's taxonomy (learning objectives), data-driven learning (learning analytics).
