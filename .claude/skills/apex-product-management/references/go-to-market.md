# Go-to-Market

## Scope
Designing how a product reaches and converts its market: motion selection (sales-led, product-led, channel), segmentation, and GTM fit.

## Core principles
- GTM motion must match deal economics: ACV under ~$5k can't afford human sales touch (self-serve/PLG territory); $25k-100k supports inside sales; $100k+ supports field sales — a motion mismatched to price point burns either CAC or conversion.
- Product-market fit and go-to-market fit are separate milestones: PMF means people want it; GTM fit means you have a repeatable, economical motion to reach them (Bob Tinker's framing) — scaling sales before the playbook repeats is the classic Series B fire.
- Beachhead discipline (Moore): dominate one segment where you have referenceable urgency before expanding; "our TAM is everyone" means your message resonates with no one and your sales team learns nothing reusable.
- Channels have power laws: most companies get one or two channels to actually work (Weinberg & Mares' Bullseye framework exists to find them fast); spreading launch budget across nine channels tests none at significance.
- The GTM math must close: CAC payback under ~18 months for venture SaaS, LTV:CAC north of 3; a motion that "works" at negative unit economics is a countdown timer, not a strategy.

## Apex practices
- Write an ICP (ideal customer profile) with disqualifiers, not just qualifiers — firmographics, trigger events, watering holes, and the "who we lose to and walk away from" list that keeps sales from chasing bad-fit logos.
- Instrument the funnel end-to-end before scaling spend: stage-by-stage conversion, cycle time, and cohort CAC by channel; scale only the channels whose cohorts retain.
- Run founder-led sales for the first 10-20 customers even when it doesn't scale — the objections, buyer language, and win themes ARE the sales playbook you'll hand to the first AEs.
- Layer motions deliberately (PLG for adoption + sales-assist for expansion is the dominant modern pattern), with explicit PQL definitions handing product signals to sales.

## Pitfalls
- Hiring a VP Sales to "figure out GTM" before any repeatable motion exists — sales leaders scale playbooks; they rarely invent them.
- Pricing/packaging finalized after the launch date is set, forcing a scramble that ships the safest (weakest) option.
- Confusing launch with GTM: a launch is an event; GTM is the standing machine — teams celebrate launch-day signups while week-4 activation quietly reads zero.

## Tools & references
Moore "Crossing the Chasm," Tinker & Weissman "Survival to Thrival," Weinberg & Mares "Traction" (Bullseye), a16z on GTM fit, OpenView PLG benchmarks, "Founding Sales" (Pete Kazanjy).
