# Personas & JTBD

## Scope
Creating user models: persona development, jobs-to-be-done framework, and using them to guide design decisions.

## Core principles
- Personas are archetypes (not real people) representing user segments — effective personas are grounded in research, not intuition.
- Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) framework shifts focus from who users are to what they're trying to accomplish — context and goals matter more than demographics.
- A job has dimensions: functional (what users want to do), emotional (how they want to feel), social (how they want to be perceived) — good products address all three.
- Personas get stale if not updated with new research; review annually and retire unused personas to avoid accumulating irrelevant archetypes.
- Overusing personas as a design crutch ("personas want X") without evidence justifies poor design — use personas to prompt discussion, not to end it.

## Apex practices
- Build personas from research data: interview findings, behavioral patterns, goals — annotate with quotes from research.
- JTBD interviews focus on the decision-making context: when did the user buy, what motivated it, what alternatives did they consider? Not satisfaction ratings.
- Create job stories (When X, I want Y, so Z) from JTBD insights — these are actionable statements for designers.
- Share personas with the team and refer to them in design reviews; periodic persona reviews prevent designs from drifting from user needs.

## Pitfalls
- Personas created by committee without research; they're just stereotypes and rarely used in practice.
- JTBD interviews that become satisfaction surveys (asking about existing solutions) instead of exploring the job being done.
- Personas that split users too finely (8+ personas); most teams can only track 3–4 personas effectively.

## Tools & references
Jobs to be Done framework (Christensen, Ramaswamy), JTBD Interview Guide, proto-personas for startup speed, persona templates (UXPin, Figma), Empathy Map Canvas; "Intercom on Jobs to be Done" (McLeod).
