# Form & Function

## Scope
The Sullivan–Bauhaus–Rams lineage: what "form follows function" actually claims, where it's true, and how to wield it without falling into sterile functionalism.

## Core principles
- Sullivan's original: "form ever follows function" was about organic unity (the shape of the eagle's wing and its flight are one fact), not about stripping ornament — his own buildings drip with it. The claim: shape should express what a thing is and does.
- Bauhaus operationalized it: start from use, material, and production method; let appearance emerge from solving those honestly. The workshop model — designers trained in materials, artists teaching craft — is the methodological core.
- Rams's ten principles compress the tradition; the keystone is "good design is as little design as possible" — but read his products: reduction stops exactly where clarity of use would suffer. The dial you can find blind is the function; the proportions that make it dignified are the form. Both are duties.
- Function includes feeling: a door handle's function is opening doors and telling your hand it's trustworthy. Emotional performance is a requirement, not a garnish (the failure of brutalist software is forgetting this).
- Honest expression beats disguise: when form contradicts function — the button that doesn't look pressable, the ornament that fights the structure — users pay the tax forever.

## Apex practices
- Write the function inventory before sketching: every job the artifact does, ranked — including social and emotional jobs — then let the top three drive the form and the rest negotiate.
- Apply Rams as a review rubric: score the design against the ten principles and force one concrete change per failing principle.
- Subtract to the clarity floor: remove elements until use suffers, add the last one back — the same stop-rule as abstraction, applied to product.
- Study the object's production: forms that fight their manufacturing (or their rendering engine, or their CSS) announce it in every detail; design with the making process, Bauhaus-style.

## Pitfalls
- "Form follows function" as a taste alibi for minimalist styling that actually hides function (mystery-meat navigation, unlabeled icons).
- Ornament-phobia: Sullivan ornamented structure to celebrate it; decoration that clarifies hierarchy or gives joy is functional.
- Function defined too narrowly — omitting learnability, pleasure, and status, then wondering why the "optimal" design loses to the delightful one.
