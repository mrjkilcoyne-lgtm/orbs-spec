# Usability Testing

## Scope
Testing designs with users: test planning, moderation, analysis, and turning findings into iterations.

## Core principles
- Usability testing reveals what users actually do vs what you predict; assumptions are often wrong — a representative sample of 5–8 users reveals 85% of problems.
- Moderated testing (in-person or remote) with a researcher observing and probing deeper is more insightful than unmoderated testing, but slower and more expensive.
- Unmoderated testing (remote, asynchronous, via UserTesting or Maze) is fast and scales to many participants but misses nuance and follow-up questions.
- Task-based testing (ask users to accomplish a goal without guidance) reveals how intuitive the design is; facilitated testing (showing features) doesn't test discoverability.
- Recording sessions (video and screen) is essential; you'll notice details in replay that you miss live.

## Apex practices
- Test early and often (wireframes, prototypes, launched features) at different stages; findings shift from navigation (early) to polish (late).
- Write task scenarios that are realistic and open-ended ("Find a product under $50" vs "Click the filters button") — let users discover how to complete tasks.
- Analyze by coding: watch recordings, highlight key quotes and behaviors, group into themes — systematic, not cherry-picked highlights.
- Close the loop: share findings with the team, iterate the design, and validate with another test — one test isn't enough to prove a change works.

## Pitfalls
- Testing only with existing users (power users, fans); they're not representative of new users or those who churn.
- Leading participants ("Do you like this design?"); ask open questions ("What do you think about this?").
- Over-interpreting single-user behaviors; patterns need at least 2–3 users to be meaningful.

## Tools & references
UserTesting, Maze, Lookback (moderated testing), Dovetail (analysis), Google Forms (quick feedback); "Don't Make Me Think" (Krug); System Usability Scale (SUS) for standardized scoring.
