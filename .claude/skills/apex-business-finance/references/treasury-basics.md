# Treasury Basics

## Scope
Managing company cash and liquidity: cash flow forecasting, banking relationships, debt management, and hedging currency or interest rate risk.

## Core principles
- Cash is king in illiquid businesses and crisis; companies optimized for profitability but not cash can run out of money and fail despite being profitable.
- Debt service requirements (principal + interest) reduce flexibility; a company with 20% EBITDA margin but $5M annual debt service is less healthy than one with 15% margin and no debt if the 20% margin is volatile.
- Working capital cycles (how fast you collect AR and sell inventory) determine cash flow timing; a 30-day customer collection cycle vs. 90-day supplier payment cycle requires 60 days of working capital buffer.
- Banking relationships matter for credit lines, merchant services, and cash management; a startup should establish a relationship with a bank early, even if not borrowing, to have it available in a crisis.
- Foreign exchange (FX) risk arises when revenue and expenses are in different currencies; a company earning revenue in euros and paying expenses in dollars faces margin compression if the dollar strengthens.

## Apex practices
- Maintain a rolling cash flow forecast (13-week rolling window, updated weekly) and a monthly cash position tracker; cash surprises should not exist.
- Negotiate terms with suppliers (net 30, net 60) and customers (net 15, net 30) to extend cash cycles; a 15-day extension on both sides can free up 1 month of cash.
- Establish a credit line (revolving) for working capital management; undrawn credit lines provide liquidity optionality at low cost.
- Hedge material FX exposure using forwards or options; if you have material revenue in EUR and expenses in USD, a quarterly FX hedge locks in margins.

## Pitfalls
- Running low on cash while technically profitable; cash flow and profitability are different (one is math, one is time).
- Holding excess cash in low-yield checking accounts; sweep cash to money market funds or short-term bonds to earn 4-5%.
- Taking on debt without understanding amortization schedules; a $10M loan at 8% with 5-year amortization costs $243k/month in debt service before interest.
