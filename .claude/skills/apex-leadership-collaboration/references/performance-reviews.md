# Performance Reviews

## Scope
Formal evaluation cycles: calibration, ratings, promotion cases, and underperformance management.

## Core principles
- The review's content must be a summary of feedback already delivered — if anything in it is news, the manager failed months earlier; reviews are for the record, not for revelation.
- Recency bias and the halo effect are the two dominant distortions: without contemporaneous notes, a review covers the last six weeks, and one salient trait (brilliant/difficult) colors every dimension.
- Calibration across managers is where fairness lives or dies: the same work must earn the same rating regardless of whose team it happened on — otherwise ratings measure manager generosity, not performance.
- Promotion committees evaluate evidence, not potential: the promotion case is won or lost in the six months of deliberately scoped, documented, next-level work before the packet is written.
- Underperformance is a system output before it's a person problem: check role clarity, feedback history, and project setup before concluding the individual is the variable (fundamental attribution error).

## Apex practices
- Keep a running brag/notes doc per report all cycle; ask reports to keep their own — memory-based reviews are recency-bias generators.
- Write the review against the published career ladder, citing specific artifacts (design docs, incidents handled, PRs, mentoring outcomes) per ladder dimension.
- In calibration, argue from evidence rows on a shared spreadsheet, and explicitly hunt for rating drift correlated with demographics or manager seniority.
- For underperformance, use a written expectations doc with concrete 30/60-day success criteria before any formal PIP — many "performance problems" evaporate when expectations become specific.

## Pitfalls
- Stack ranking with forced distribution in small teams — with n=6, forcing a "below expectations" manufactures injustice and teaches the team to compete instead of collaborate.
- Letting compensation math drive the rating instead of the reverse ("we can only afford two 'exceeds' this cycle").
- Writing promotion feedback as "keep doing what you're doing" — no actionable gap means no path, and your best people leave to find one.
