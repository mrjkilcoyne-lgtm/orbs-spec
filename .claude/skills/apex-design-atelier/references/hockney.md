# Hockney

## Scope
David Hockney's sixty-year argument that seeing is joyful and tools are liberating: the pools, the joiners, the iPad — adopting new media without losing the hand.

## Core principles
- Embrace the new tool early and completely: Polaroids, fax machines, iPhones, iPads — Hockney treats each new medium as a fresh set of eyes, not a threat to craft. The lesson for anyone whose tools keep changing: master the constraint, don't mourn the old one.
- The joiners' insight: a single photograph is one frozen eye-blink; a grid of overlapping photos ("Pearblossom Highway") reconstructs how looking actually works — accumulated glances over time. Interfaces and data stories can do the same: composite partial views that admit their seams.
- Flat color as light: the LA pools render blinding sunshine with flat acrylic planes and one wiggling splash — light conveyed by color relationships, not rendering. Economy beats simulation when the color logic is right.
- Space you can walk through: Hockney's reverse perspective and moving focus put the viewer inside the scene rather than at a peephole. Design corollary: layouts as environments to move through, not pictures to stare at.
- Joy is a serious position: against the cool irony of his era, Hockney committed to pleasure — pools, lovers, spring blossoms. Work made with visible delight transmits delight; that's a craft property, not sentimentality.

## Apex practices
- Do the joiner exercise with real material: build one composite from many partial captures (screenshots, photos, chart fragments) with honest overlaps — learn what seams add that seamlessness hides.
- Draw daily on the current device at its native fidelity (Hockney's iPad flowers, sent unfinished-fresh to friends every morning): fluency comes from volume in the actual medium, not from tutorials about it.
- Push flat-color rendering: pick a light situation and hit it with ≤8 flat swatches, no gradients — trains the color-relationship muscle every screen design uses.
- Revisit a subject serially (Hockney painted the same road through four seasons): iteration on one motif exposes what your style actually is.

## Pitfalls
- Tool-hopping without depth — Hockney spends years per medium; the novelty is the entry, not the work.
- Confusing flat with simple: those pool planes sit on precise drawing and razor color decisions.
- Irony as default: hedged, self-deprecating design ages worse than committed warmth.

## Tools & references
"A Bigger Splash," "Pearblossom Highway," "A Bigger Grand Canyon," the iPad spring drawings, "Secret Knowledge" and "A History of Pictures" (Hockney & Gayford).
