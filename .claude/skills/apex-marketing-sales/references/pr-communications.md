# PR & Communications

## Scope
Managing media relationships, securing earned media coverage, crisis communication, and announcement strategy.

## Core principles
- Earned media (press coverage) has more credibility than paid media; a headline in TechCrunch is worth 10x the equivalent ad spend in terms of trust.
- Press coverage takes months to arrange; pitching a journalist with a story fit for their beat (not your talking points) gets 10x response rate vs. mass pitching.
- Crisis communication requires speed and transparency; first statement should acknowledge the issue, not deny or deflect. Delayed responses destroy credibility.
- Newsworthiness is relative to the publication; "we raised $10M" is newsworthy for a startup but not for the Wall Street Journal. Target publications where your news is actually news.
- Story placement strategy varies by stage: Series A is newsworthy (funding), Series B/C less so, but product launches are always newsworthy if they represent meaningful customer impact.

## Apex practices
- Build relationships with 5-10 journalists who cover your space; regular contact (share insights, offer founder for interviews) without pitching builds rapport.
- When pitching, lead with the story angle for the journalist's audience (not your benefit); e.g., "enterprise companies are shifting from waterfall to agile — here's the data" angles better than "our product does agile."
- Create a crisis communication plan (roles, rapid response team, holding statements) before you need it; you can't think clearly when a crisis hits.
- Share company milestones (hiring, new office, customer wins) through multiple channels (email, social, press release); don't rely on press to amplify.

## Pitfalls
- Pitching generic "company news" that has no story angle; journalists get 500+ pitches per day and filter for actual news.
- Responding defensively to critical coverage; gracious, factual responses build credibility more than arguing with critics.
- Announcing major news via press release without context or journalist relationships; the same news gets 100x more coverage if 3 journalists already know the story.
