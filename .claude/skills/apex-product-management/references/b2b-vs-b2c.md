# B2B vs B2C

## Scope
Fundamental differences in buying, retention, and product strategy between business and consumer products.

## Core principles
- B2B products are sold, not bought: the user (engineer, analyst) is often not the buyer (finance, IT); misaligned incentives require a dual product (make the user's job easier, make the buyer's life cheaper).
- B2C activation is measured in minutes, retention in daily/weekly returns; B2B activation is measured in days (implementation, first value), retention in whether the contract renews and expands.
- Enterprise buyers have RFP cycles, procurement processes, and multi-stakeholder sign-off; shipping fast matters less than shipping something that passes security, compliance, and ROI scrutiny.
- B2B economics invert CAC and LTV scales: a $5k/year B2B customer is worth 4 years of LTV; a consumer app earning $5/user needs 1000x the adoption to amortize a $5k engineering investment.
- B2C products accumulate habit loops (notification, social proof, FOMO); B2B products accumulate stickiness through embedding (the service becomes part of the customer's critical path).

## Apex practices
- In B2B, nail the buyer and user separately: e.g., Slack is irresistible to teams (user love) but sold on email integration and compliance (buyer rationalization).
- B2C roadmaps chase engagement metrics (daily active users, session length); B2B roadmaps chase expansion metrics (contract value growth, customer effort score, upsell velocity).
- Enterprise requires a dedicated customer success function with SLAs; consumer products are self-service or have light concierge for whales — a heavy CS team is not scalable at $100/user.
- B2C requires defensibility via network effects or habit; B2B defensibility comes from switching costs (data lock-in, integration depth, training sunk).

## Pitfalls
- Using consumer metrics (DAU, session length) to measure B2B success; contract expansion is the real signal.
- Assuming enterprise sales require a large sales team at day one; land-and-expand (inbound from users) can work for infrastructure.
- Underestimating implementation friction in B2B; free trials need either no integration (Stripe webhooks work immediately) or a co-sell implementation partner.

## Tools & references
SaaS metrics (CAC payback, NRR — look at Hubspot or Looker benchmarks), PLG playbook (Hockeystack, OpenView), enterprise buying cycles (JEDI, RFP templates), Crossing the Chasm (B2B adoption s-curves).
