# Sales Process

## Scope
Structured selling: pipeline stages, forecasting, deal management, and closing discipline.

## Core principles
- A sales process is a series of discrete stages (prospect, qualified lead, negotiation, close) with criteria for advancement; without stages, deals stall and forecasting is impossible.
- Pipeline visibility requires discipline: CRM entries must be updated in real time, not back-filled at month-end; a stale CRM is noise.
- Average deal size and sales cycle determine team size; if average deal is $5k with 30-day cycle, a rep needs 20+ deals in-flight and can close 4-5 per month. If average deal is $500k with 6-month cycle, a rep needs 10-15 deals and closes 1-2 per month.
- Forecasting accuracy improves with process; "I'll close it" forecasts are noise. "I sent a proposal, waiting for legal review" is signal (the deal is likely to close, with known risk).
- Sales velocity (time from first contact to close) varies by product and customer size; tracking velocity per segment shows which segments are efficient and which are money-traps.

## Apex practices
- Define your sales stages with exit criteria (prospect → qualified requires initial discovery call; qualified → proposal requires budget confirmation; proposal → close requires legal sign-off).
- Implement CRM discipline: every interaction logged, every deal's next step documented, every deal's close date with reasoning (calendar reservation, customer timeline, internal deadline).
- Forecast on a pipeline basis (deals in each stage × close rate for that stage) not on rep gut-feel; a rep who says "I'll close $500k this quarter" provides no signal; "I have $1M in deals in proposal stage and 50% historical close rate" is predictive.
- Run weekly pipeline reviews (which deals are progressing, which are stalled, what needs attention); this is where forecasting accuracy compounds.

## Pitfalls
- Allowing reps to inflate forecasts (deals that won't close stay in the pipeline until the last day, then marked "lost"); this destroys forecast credibility and hides real problems.
- Missing the sales process opportunity (ops, finance, support all sign up) and fragmenting efforts; sales, ops, and customer success must operate in concert.
- Shortening the sales cycle artificially by discounting; you're not shortening, you're destroying economics.

## Tools & references
Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive (CRM), Gong or Chorus (call recording and analysis), The Sandler Method, MEDDIC sales methodology, Winning by Jack Welch (Ch. 7 on sales).
