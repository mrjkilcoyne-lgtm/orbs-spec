# Public Speaking

## Scope
Delivering presentations effectively: structure, delivery, managing anxiety, and engaging audiences in real time.

## Core principles
- The rule of three: people remember concepts better in triplets (three points, three examples). Presentations with 3-5 key ideas stick; presentations with 20 are noise.
- Your body language broadcasts belief; if you don't believe what you're saying, the audience won't. Stand confidently, maintain eye contact, let your hands move naturally.
- Silence is powerful; pauses after key points let them land. Rushing through dilutes impact. Aim for 1 pause per minute.
- Slides are visual aids, not scripts; if every word is on the slide, you're reading to them, not presenting. Slides should have images, minimal text, and reinforce your voice.
- Anxiety is normal; experienced speakers still get nervous. Channel it into energy and focus. Breathing (4-count in, 6-count out) calms the nervous system.

## Apex practices
- Practice out loud, not in your head; timing is different, stumbles become apparent, muscle memory builds.
- Start strong: a question, surprising statistic, or personal story beats "Hi, I'm... and today I'll talk about..."
- Know your first 30 seconds and your last 30 seconds by heart; extemporize the middle. This anchors you and prevents blank panic.
- Engage the audience: ask rhetorical questions, take a poll, invite a volunteer. Interaction wakes people up and makes them co-authors of meaning.

## Pitfalls
- Reading slides verbatim; audiences can read faster than you speak, so you're wasting time and sounding boring.
- Apologizing for nerves or mistakes; audiences don't notice until you draw attention to it. Keep going.
- Packing too much into the time; better to deep-dive on one point than skim five. People forget 90% anyway; make sure it's the right 10%.

## Tools & references
Carmine Gallo's "Talk Like TED," Nancy Duarte's "Resonate," presentation skills courses (Toastmasters), video review (watch yourself present).
