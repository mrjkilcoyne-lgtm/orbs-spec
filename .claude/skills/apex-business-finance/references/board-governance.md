# Board Governance

## Scope
Board composition, meeting structure, decision-making authority, and fiduciary duties of directors.

## Core principles
- The board has a fiduciary duty to shareholders; directors can be held personally liable for gross negligence or self-dealing. Taking a board seat means accepting legal exposure.
- Board composition (founders, independent directors, investor directors) shapes decision-making; an all-founder board rubber-stamps decisions; an all-independent board can slow strategy.
- Board authority is defined by bylaws and the law of incorporation; the board typically hires/fires the CEO, approves major capex, financing, and material contracts, and discloses to shareholders.
- Investor directors have contractual board seats (Series A term sheet typically grants one seat, board observation rights, and major decision approval rights); founder control and investor control are often in tension.
- Meeting frequency (quarterly standard) and agenda discipline (consent agenda for routine items, focused discussion on key decisions) determine board effectiveness; rambling meetings waste time; undiscussed critical issues create liability.

## Apex practices
- Use a board portal (Diligent, Nasdaq Boardvantage) to share materials in advance (3 days minimum); directors should never be surprised on the call with new information.
- Run a brief CEO executive session at the end of each board meeting (directors without the CEO and management) to discuss CEO performance, board dynamics, and sensitive topics.
- Conduct an annual board self-evaluation (anonymous survey of board effectiveness, areas for improvement) and discuss findings; this catches dysfunction before it becomes a crisis.
- Maintain detailed board minutes (what was discussed, who decided what, key dissents) for legal protection; vague minutes and no dissent record can be evidence of negligence.

## Pitfalls
- Stacking the board with yes-men; a dissenting voice is uncomfortable but necessary. Advisors should be advisory, not decision-makers trying to minimize disagreement.
- Allowing investor directors to override founder vision; board governance works when there's clarity on where investors have veto power (major acquisitions, funding, CEO replacement) vs. where founders have operating discretion.
- Failing to disclose conflicts of interest (a director has a personal stake in a decision); recusal and disclosure protect both the director and the company legally.

## Tools & references
Delaware General Corporation Law (most tech companies incorporate in DE), NASB (National Association of Secretaries of Business) governance standards, Diligent board portal, corporate law firms (Cooley, Fenwick, WSGR), director insurance (D&O insurance).
