# Discovery Calls

## Scope
Initial sales conversations with prospects: qualifying buyers, uncovering pain points, and understanding decision-making authority.

## Core principles
- Discovery is about listening, not pitching; if the rep talks >30%, the rep is selling, not discovering. Questions should outnumber statements 5:1.
- Qualifying is binary: does this prospect have a problem you solve, budget to solve it, and authority to decide? If the answer is "no" to any, end the call early and redirect to the right person.
- Pain articulation by the prospect is gold; if the prospect says "we waste 10 hours per week on this process," that justifies premium pricing. If you have to convince them there's pain, there's no deal.
- Decision authority is critical: talking to an implementer who doesn't control budget is a waste of time. Map the decision committee early (who decides, who influences, who implements, who pays).
- Objections in discovery ("we have something similar already") are gold; they show what you're up against. Addressing them now builds trust and surfaces the real buying criteria.

## Apex practices
- Open with a clear agenda (30-min call to understand if there's a fit for both sides, not a product demo) and confirm availability; this signals respect and professionalism.
- Use the MEDDIC framework: Metrics (what does success look like?), Economic buyer (who pays?), Decision criteria (what matters most?), Decision process (how do they decide?), Identify pain, Champion (who advocates for you internally?).
- Ask open-ended questions ("tell me about your current process") before closed questions ("are you happy with your current vendor?"); open questions reveal priorities.
- Take notes visibly and ask clarifying questions; this signals the prospect's input matters and prevents you from misunderstanding.

## Pitfalls
- Pitching product in discovery; discovery is not the time to show the product. Product comes after you understand the problem.
- Failing to qualify out; a 60-minute discovery call with an unqualified prospect is a waste. It's ok to say "I don't think we're a fit."
- Not mapping decision-making; talking to an enthusiast with no authority means the deal is held hostage by their inability to decide internally.

## Tools & references
MEDDIC framework (Jack Quarles), Sandler training, Jill Konrath "Selling to Big Companies," Call recording (Gong, Chorus) for coaching, Zig Ziglar "Secrets of Closing the Sale."
